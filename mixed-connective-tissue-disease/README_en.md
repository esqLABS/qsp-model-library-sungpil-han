## Overview
Mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD) is an autoimmune connective tissue disease in which the clinical features of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), systemic sclerosis (SSc), and polymyositis (PM) overlap, with anti-U1-RNP antibody positivity as an essential serological marker. Prevalence is rare, at about 3~7 per 100,000, and Raynaud phenomenon is almost universal (95%). The core pathogenesis is multi-organ inflammation triggered by anti-U1-RNP antibodies and associated T cell immune abnormalities, with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), interstitial lung disease (ILD), and pulmonary fibrosis as the major complications determining long-term prognosis. Treatment is based on steroids · hydroxychloroquine · MMF, with the addition of endothelin receptor antagonists (bosentan, etc.) and PDE5 inhibitors when PAH is present.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Anti-U1-RNP antibody production | Dual T cell/B cell activation against U1 snRNP, IFN-α signalling | High serum anti-U1-RNP titre |
| T cell abnormality | Increased Th1 (IFN-γ) · Th17 (IL-17), decreased Treg | Multi-organ inflammation |
| Vascular lesions | Increased ET-1 · decreased eNOS · endothelial cell damage → Raynaud/PAH | Pulmonary vascular remodelling |
| Pulmonary fibrosis | Myofibroblast activation by TGF-β · IL-6 | Decreased FVC · DLCO |
| Myositis | IFN-γ-mediated muscle fibre damage, elevated CK | Decreased muscle strength · reduced MMT |
| Arthritis | Synovial inflammation by TNF-α · IL-6 · IL-17 | Arthralgia, arthritis |
| Complement consumption | Decreased C3 · C4, immune complex-mediated complement activation | Overlap with SLE features |

## Drug Targets
- **Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ)**: TLR7/9 inhibition → suppresses IFN-α production, long-term background treatment (all patients)
- **Prednisolone**: suppresses TNF-α · IL-6 · IFN-γ, controls myositis · arthritis · serositis
- **Mycophenolate (MMF/MPA)**: inhibits B cell/T cell proliferation → decreased antibody production, for ILD/nephritis
- **Rituximab**: B cell depletion, for severe or refractory cases
- **Bosentan** (endothelin receptor antagonist ETA/ETB): blocks ET-1 signalling → reduces pulmonary vascular resistance (when PAH is present)
- **Tacrolimus**: calcineurin inhibition to modulate T cells when myositis or nephritis is present

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (HCQ 3 compartments, MPA/MMF 3 compartments + enterohepatic recirculation, prednisolone 2 compartments, rituximab 2 compartments, bosentan 2 compartments) + disease PD compartments (Th1, Th17, Treg, naive B cells, GC B cells, plasma cells, anti-U1-RNP antibody, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17, IFN-γ, TGF-β, IFN-α, ET-1, pulmonary vascular resistance, collagen/fibrosis, FVC, DLCO, CK, MMT, joint swelling index, C3, C4)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history, ② HCQ + low-dose steroid, ③ HCQ + MMF + steroid, ④ rituximab + MMF (severe), ⑤ addition of bosentan (HCQ + bosentan when PAH is present)
- **Calibration/rationale**: qualitative reference to the EULAR/ERA-EDTA guidelines for PAH accompanying connective tissue disease, major SLE/SSc cohorts (studies analogous to INPULSIS-MCTD), and PARIS registry data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (anti-U1-RNP titre · baseline FVC · DLCO · PAP · muscle strength · joint swelling count), drug PK concentration trends (HCQ · MMF · rituximab · bosentan), key PD biomarkers (cytokines · antibodies · lung function), clinical endpoints (change in FVC/DLCO · 6-minute walk distance · MYOACT), treatment scenario comparison, and long-term lung function prediction.
