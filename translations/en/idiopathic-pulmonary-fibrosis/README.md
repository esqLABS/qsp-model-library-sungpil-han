## Overview

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease in which an aberrant repair response after alveolar epithelial injury activates fibroblasts/myofibroblasts and deposits excessive ECM (extracellular matrix), irreversibly destroying lung parenchyma. Median survival is only about 3~5 years after diagnosis, and annual incidence is about 10~20 per 100,000. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and TGF-β1 are the key drivers, with a vicious cycle of AEC2 (type II alveolar epithelial cell) injury → TGF-β signalling → fibroblast activation → collagen deposition causing progressive decline in FVC and DLCO. Nintedanib and pirfenidone are currently the only antifibrotic agents able to slow the rate of progression.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Alveolar epithelial injury | Ageing · smoking · genetics (MUC5B · TERT) · environment → AEC2 injury/ER stress | Repeated microinjury |
| TGF-β1 signalling activation | AEC2 injury → TGF-β1 secretion → Smad2/3 → fibroblast activation/EMT | Fibroblast proliferation |
| M2 macrophage polarisation | TGF-β · IL-4 · IL-13 → M2 macrophages → suppression of antifibrotic activity/additional TGF-β secretion | Feedback promoting fibrosis |
| ROS oxidative stress | Mitochondrial dysfunction · NADPH oxidase → excess ROS → additional AEC2 injury | Accelerated fibrosis |
| Myofibroblast differentiation | TGF-β → α-SMA+ myofibroblasts → contraction/mass collagen synthesis | ECM deposition, lung stiffening |
| MMP/TIMP imbalance | Excess TIMP → MMP inhibition → decreased ECM degradation → collagen accumulation | Destruction of lung structure, decreased diffusion capacity |

## Drug Targets

- **Pirfenidone**: multiple mechanisms — TGF-β signal inhibition, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory; demonstrated to suppress FVC decline in the ASCEND/CAPACITY phase 3 trials
- **Nintedanib**: simultaneous inhibition of VEGFR · FGFR · PDGFR (triple tyrosine kinase inhibitor) → blocks fibroblast proliferation/migration; evidence from the INPULSIS phase 3 trials
- **Pirfenidone+nintedanib combination**: complementary mechanisms; additional FVC-preservation effect versus monotherapy under investigation
- **Direct TGF-β1 blockade (e.g. bintrafusp alfa)**: in development; blocks upstream of the Smad pathway
- **LPA1 receptor antagonist (BMS-986020)**: inhibits fibroblast migration; phase 2 clinical data exist
- **Antifibrotic combination strategies**: IL-13/IL-4Ra blockade (lebrikizumab), galunisertib (TGFβ-RI inhibitor) under investigation

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: includes drug PK (pirfenidone gut/central/peripheral, nintedanib gut/central/peripheral) + disease PD (AEC2, TGF-β1, M2 macrophages, ROS, activated fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, collagen, MMP, TIMP) + clinical marker compartments (FVC%, DLCO%)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① placebo (natural history), ② pirfenidone 801 mg TID, ③ nintedanib 150 mg BID, ④ pirfenidone+nintedanib combination, ⑤ low-dose pirfenidone 267 mg TID
- **Calibration/rationale**: qualitatively calibrated the FVC/DLCO decline curves based on data from ASCEND (King 2014, NEJM) — annual FVC decline −235 mL → −125 mL, and INPULSIS (Richeldi 2014, NEJM) — annual FVC decline −223 mL → −115 mL

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of tabs for patient profile (baseline FVC%, DLCO%, smoking history, GAP score), drug PK kinetics (plasma concentrations of pirfenidone/nintedanib), TGF-β/fibrosis PD markers (collagen/myofibroblasts), pulmonary function clinical endpoints (FVC%/DLCO% trends), comparison of 5 treatment scenarios (comparison of annual FVC decline), and biomarkers (TGF-β1 · MMP-7 · SP-D).
