## Overview

Gout is a metabolic disease in which supersaturation of serum uric acid leads to deposition of MSU (monosodium urate) crystals within joints, with repeated acute inflammatory arthritis flares driven by the NLRP3 inflammasome-IL-1β axis. Worldwide adult prevalence is about 1~4%, and it is the most common inflammatory arthritis in Western countries. Both uric acid overproduction (excess purine metabolism) and decreased renal excretion (URAT1, ABCG2 mutations) cause hyperuricaemia, and chronic disease is accompanied by tophus formation and renal damage. Urate-lowering agents (allopurinol, febuxostat) and acute-flare suppressants (colchicine, NSAIDs, IL-1 blockers) form the two pillars of treatment.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Purine metabolism/XO pathway | Xanthine oxidase (XO) → uric acid overproduction; URAT1/ABCG2 transporter mutations → decreased excretion | Elevated serum uric acid (>6.8 mg/dL) |
| MSU crystal formation | Supersaturation → nucleation; crystallisation depending on temperature, pH, and binding protein conditions | Crystal deposition within joints |
| NLRP3 inflammasome activation | MSU → TLR2/4 signalling + NLRP3 assembly → caspase-1 → IL-1β maturation/secretion | Acute gout flare |
| Neutrophil recruitment | IL-1β · TNF-α → upregulation of E-selectin/ICAM-1 → neutrophil infiltration | Joint swelling, pain, fever |
| Tophus formation | Chronic MSU accumulation → macrophage/fibroblast response → tophus | Joint damage, urolithiasis |
| Renal impairment | Hyperuricaemia → tubular crystal deposition → sustained cytokines | Decreased eGFR |

## Drug Targets

- **Allopurinol**: competitive XO inhibition → decreased uric acid production; converted to oxypurinol for sustained inhibition
- **Febuxostat**: non-competitive XO inhibitor; an alternative to allopurinol in patients with decreased renal function
- **Lesinurad/Benzbromarone**: URAT1 inhibition → promotes renal excretion of uric acid
- **Colchicine**: inhibits tubulin polymerisation → blocks neutrophil migration; prevention/treatment of acute flares
- **NSAIDs (e.g. indomethacin)**: COX-1/2 inhibition → decreased prostaglandins; first-line treatment for acute flares
- **Anakinra/Canakinumab**: IL-1 receptor antagonist / anti-IL-1β monoclonal antibody; for refractory flares

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: includes uric acid PK (gut/central/peripheral/synovial compartments), MSU crystal/tophus, inflammatory mediators (IL-1β · TNF-α · neutrophils · pain · joint damage · eGFR), drug PK (allopurinol/oxypurinol, febuxostat, lesinurad, colchicine, indomethacin, anakinra, canakinumab-IL-1β complex)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated baseline, ② allopurinol 300 mg/day, ③ febuxostat 80 mg/day, ④ allopurinol+lesinurad combination, ⑤ colchicine acute-flare protocol, ⑥ indomethacin acute flare, ⑦ canakinumab 150 mg SC (refractory flare), ⑧ febuxostat+colchicine long-term prophylaxis
- **Calibration/rationale**: qualitatively calibrated the curve for achieving the uric acid target (<6 mg/dL) based on clinical trial data from CONFIRM/CONFIRMS (febuxostat), the ACR 2012 gout guidelines, Tardif et al. COLCOT, and others

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of tabs for patient profile settings (baseline serum uric acid, eGFR, comorbidities), uric acid PK kinetics, crystal/tophus formation PD, inflammatory markers (IL-1β · CRP · neutrophils), acute flares and clinical endpoints (flare frequency, joint damage), comparison of 8 treatment scenarios, and biomarkers (serum uric acid, eGFR trends).
