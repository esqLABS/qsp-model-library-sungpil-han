## Overview

Systemic sclerosis (scleroderma) is a rare connective-tissue disease combining vasculopathy, autoimmune activation, and progressive fibrosis, with a global prevalence estimated at about 150-300 per million. Excess collagen deposition across multiple organs — skin, lungs, heart, kidneys, gastrointestinal tract — causes irreversible organ damage. Its core pathogenesis can be summarised as a chain: endothelial injury → excess TGF-β/IL-6 secretion → fibroblast activation → myofibroblast conversion → ECM accumulation. Current treatment strategy rests on three pillars: immunosuppression (mycophenolate, tocilizumab), antifibrotic therapy (nintedanib), and vasodilation (bosentan, iloprost).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecule/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Vascular endothelial injury | Anti-endothelial-cell antibodies, elevated endothelin-1, reduced NO/PGI2 | Raynaud's phenomenon, digital ulcers, PAH |
| Excess TGF-β secretion | Fibroblast TGF-βRII overexpression, Smad2/3 phosphorylation | Increased collagen I/III synthesis, ECM accumulation |
| IL-6/Th17 axis | IL-6 autocrine loop, Th17:Treg imbalance | Systemic inflammation, autoantibody production |
| Myofibroblast activation | Fibroblast activation → α-SMA+ myofibroblast conversion, PDGFR/FGFR signalling | Skin and lung fibrosis (rising mRSS, declining FVC) |
| Pulmonary vascular remodelling | ↑ ET-1, increased PVR, RV hypertrophy | Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), shortened 6MWT |
| B-cell autoimmunity | Anti-Scl-70, anti-ACA antibodies, B-cell activation | Immune-complex deposition, amplified organ damage |

## Drug Targets

- **FGFR/PDGFR/VEGFR inhibitor** — Nintedanib: inhibits fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis (SENSCIS trial)
- **IL-6R blocker** — Tocilizumab: blocks IL-6 signalling, preserves FVC (faSScinate, focuSSced trials)
- **T-cell/B-cell suppressant** — Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF/MPA): immunosuppression, stabilises skin/lung disease
- **Endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA)** — Bosentan: blocks ET-1, prevents digital ulcers (RAPIDS-1/2)
- **Prostacyclin analogue** — Iloprost (inhaled): PGI2-like action, reduces PVR (STEP trial)

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (nintedanib 2-compartment oral, tocilizumab 2-compartment IV, MMF/MPA 1-compartment, bosentan 1-compartment, inhaled iloprost 1-compartment) + disease PD compartments (TGF-β, IL-6, Th17, Bnv, fibroblast activation, myofibroblasts, Col1/Col3, ECM, mRSS, FVC, DLCO, ET-1, NO, PGI2, endothelial integrity, PVR, 6MWD), roughly 30 compartments in total
- **Key treatment scenarios**: (1) nintedanib 150 mg BID, (2) tocilizumab 8 mg/kg IV q4w, (3) MMF 3,000 mg/day, (4) bosentan 125 mg BID, (5) nintedanib + tocilizumab + MMF combination
- **Calibration/rationale**: parameters calibrated against data from the SENSCIS trial (nintedanib, FVC preservation), faSScinate/focuSSced (tocilizumab, mRSS), RAPIDS-1/2 (bosentan, digital ulcers), and the STEP trial (iloprost)

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 or more tabs: a patient profile (weight, disease subtype, baseline mRSS/FVC/PVR settings), PK visualisation (plasma concentration-time curves for each drug), fibrosis PD indices (mRSS/FVC/DLCO time series), vascular PD indices (ET-1/PVR/6MWD), treatment-scenario comparison (monotherapy vs. combination therapy), and a biomarker panel (TGF-β, IL-6, Th17).
