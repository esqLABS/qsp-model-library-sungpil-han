## Overview

Guillain-Barré syndrome is an autoimmune neuropathy in which an immune response to a preceding infection (most often *Campylobacter jejuni*, CMV, or EBV) generates anti-ganglioside antibodies (anti-GM1, anti-GD1a, anti-GQ1b) against peripheral nerves via molecular mimicry, and complement-mediated axonal or myelin injury then produces acute flaccid paralysis. Annual incidence is about 1–2 per 100,000, with onset 2–4 weeks after the preceding infection. Its two major subtypes — AIDP (acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, more common in Western countries) and AMAN (acute motor axonal neuropathy, more common in Asia) — differ in pathogenesis and prognosis. IVIG and plasma exchange are the only treatments of proven efficacy, and mechanical ventilation is required in severe cases.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Molecular mimicry / anti-ganglioside antibodies | *Campylobacter* LOS-GM1 cross-reactivity → B cells/plasma cells → IgG/IgM anti-ganglioside antibodies | Rising antibody titres |
| Complement activation / MAC formation | Antibody-ganglioside binding → classical pathway → C3b → C5a + MAC (C5b-9) | Damage to myelin or axonal membrane |
| AIDP myelin injury | Antibody + complement → attack on the Schwann cell membrane → demyelination → conduction block | Limb paralysis, sensory loss |
| AMAN axonal injury | Anti-GM1/GD1a + MAC → injury at the node of Ranvier → axonal degeneration | Motor paralysis, delayed recovery |
| T-cell inflammatory response | Th1/Th17 → IFN-γ/IL-17 → macrophage nerve infiltration | Promotes demyelination and axonal destruction |
| Respiratory muscle involvement | Ascending paralysis → weakness of the diaphragm and accessory respiratory muscles | Reduced FVC, need for mechanical ventilation |

## Drug Targets

- **Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: blocks antibody-mediated nerve injury via Fc receptor saturation, complement inhibition, and anti-idiotypic action; standard treatment (0.4 g/kg × 5 days)
- **Plasmapheresis (PE)**: directly removes circulating anti-ganglioside antibodies; an alternative to IVIG
- **Eculizumab**: an anti-C5 monoclonal antibody → blocks MAC formation; used experimentally in AMAN, with preclinical efficacy confirmed
- **Supportive care**: mechanical ventilation (FVC < 20 mL/kg), cardiac monitoring, rehabilitation

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: includes pathogen/DC/macrophage activation, B cells/plasma cells, anti-GM1/GD1a/GQ1b antibodies, T cells (Th1, Th17, Treg), cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10), complement (C3b, MAC, C5a), myelin injury/axonal injury/nerve function, GBS score/Hughes grade/FVC%, IVIG PK (central/peripheral), plasma exchange effect, and eculizumab PK compartments
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated AIDP baseline, ② early IVIG (day 7), ③ delayed IVIG (day 14), ④ plasma exchange, ⑤ IVIG + eculizumab, ⑥ untreated AMAN
- **Calibration/rationale**: the Hughes-grade recovery curve was qualitatively calibrated against data from the Dutch GBS Study Group, the IGOS cohort, Halstead et al. (eculizumab preclinical data, Brain 2008), and Dutch plasma exchange trials

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of a patient profile tab (preceding infection, AIDP/AMAN subtype, baseline Hughes grade, antibody subtype), IVIG/plasma exchange PK simulation, antibody/complement/T-cell PD metrics, nerve function and GBS clinical endpoints (Hughes grade, GBS score, FVC%), comparison across 6 treatment scenarios, and a biomarker tab (NfL, anti-ganglioside titres).
