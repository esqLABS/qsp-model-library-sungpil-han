## Overview
Bullous pemphigoid (BP) is the most common autoimmune blistering disease,
in which autoantibodies (IgG, IgE) against BP180 (collagen XVII) and BP230
at the dermal-epidermal junction (DEJ) cause subepidermal blisters. It
occurs mainly in people over 70 (prevalence roughly 20-40 per 100,000), and
the core mechanisms of tissue damage are Th2-skewed immunity, eosinophil
infiltration, mast cell activation, and complement C5a-mediated neutrophil
recruitment. Standard treatment is systemic corticosteroids; doxycycline
plus topical steroids, dupilumab, omalizumab, and rituximab are used to
reduce adverse effects in elderly patients.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways
| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Th2 skewing and B-cell activation | IL-4/IL-13 -> class switching -> IgE/IgG generation | Generation of anti-BP180/BP230 autoantibodies |
| Anti-BP180 IgG complement activation | C1q activation -> C3b -> C5a generation | Recruitment of neutrophils/eosinophils to the DEJ |
| Anti-BP180 IgE mast cell activation | FcεRI crosslinking -> degranulation -> histamine | Pruritus, urticarial prodromal lesions |
| Eosinophil infiltration | IL-5, eotaxin -> eosinophil MBP/ECP release | Further DEJ damage, blister expansion |
| Protease-mediated DEJ damage | MMP-9, NE -> BP180 cleavage | Subepidermal blister formation |
| Long-lived plasma cells | Bone-marrow LLPC -> sustained autoantibody production | Relapse and incomplete remission |

## Key Drug Targets
- **Prednisolone (high-dose taper)**: broad anti-inflammatory action -> rapid blister suppression — standard first-line therapy (BLISTER trial)
- **Low-dose prednisolone + doxycycline**: MMP inhibition, anti-inflammatory — corticosteroid-sparing in elderly patients
- **Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα)**: blocks IL-4/IL-13 -> reduces Th2/IgE — biologic option (multiple case reports)
- **Omalizumab (anti-IgE)**: neutralises free IgE -> suppresses mast cell/eosinophil activation — for IgE-high BP
- **Rituximab (anti-CD20)**: depletes B cells/plasma cells -> reduces autoantibodies — for refractory/relapsing BP

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK (prednisolone 3-compartment, dupilumab
  SC 2-compartment, omalizumab SC 2-compartment, rituximab, doxycycline
  2-compartment), immune PD (naive/activated/memory/SLPC/LLPC B cells,
  Th2, anti-BP180 IgG/IgE, blood eosinophils, skin eosinophils, mast
  cells, complement C5a, DEJ integrity)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① no treatment, ② high-dose prednisolone
  taper, ③ low-dose prednisolone + doxycycline, ④ dupilumab + low-dose
  prednisolone, ⑤ omalizumab + low-dose prednisolone, ⑥ rituximab 1g x2 +
  short-course steroids
- **Calibration/basis**: reference to major BP clinical trials such as the
  BLISTER trial (doxycycline) and MVP (bertilimumab), and to antibody
  titre/BPDAI score data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile (age, baseline IgE, eosinophils,
BPDAI), pharmacokinetics (plasma concentration of each drug), immune PD
(B-cell fractions, IgG/IgE antibody titres, eosinophils), skin damage
(DEJ integrity, blister formation, BPDAI), scenario comparison (6
scenarios), and relapse-prediction biomarkers.
