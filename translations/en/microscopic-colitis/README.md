## Overview
Microscopic colitis (MC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease characterised by chronic or intermittent watery, non-bloody diarrhoea, in which colonoscopy findings are normal or nearly normal but biopsy reveals characteristic abnormalities. Its two main subtypes are **lymphocytic colitis (LC; CD8+ intraepithelial lymphocytes ≥ 20/100 EC)** and **collagenous colitis (CC; subepithelial collagen band ≥ 10 μm)**. It is common in women over 60, and exposure to drugs such as NSAIDs/PPIs/SSRIs is a strong triggering factor. It is frequently associated with coeliac disease and autoimmune thyroiditis, and budesonide MMX is the standard treatment.

## Key Pathophysiology (Key Pathways)
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Drug triggers | NSAIDs (COX, mucosal injury), PPIs (bile acid/microbiome perturbation), SSRIs (mucosal 5-HT) | 2–7-fold increase in disease risk |
| Th1/CD8+ IEL immunity | IFN-γ, IL-15, T-bet-mediated intraepithelial lymphocyte proliferation | Increased epithelial cell apoptosis |
| Cytokine storm | TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17A, IL-23 | Reduced barrier proteins (claudin/ZO-1) |
| Collagen band deposition (CC) | TGF-β/MMP-9 imbalance in pericryptal myofibroblasts, type I/III/VI collagen | Thickening of the subepithelial collagen band |
| Bile acid malabsorption | Reduced ileal ASBT function, decreased FGF19, increased CYP7A1 | Increased colonic bile acid load → secretory diarrhoea |
| Altered mucosal transport | Decreased NHE3, increased NKCC1, CFTR/cAMP activation | Shift from absorption to secretion |
| Microbiome changes | Decreased F. prausnitzii/Akkermansia, increased Proteobacteria | Decreased SCFA, increased LPS |
| Weakened mucosal barrier | Changes in TJ proteins, MUC2, and Paneth cell defensins | Antigen passage → immune activation |

## Drug Targets
- **Budesonide MMX** — local GR activation, suppression of NF-κB transcription; the induction/maintenance standard
- **Cholestyramine / colesevelam** — bile acid sequestration, effective in patients with concurrent BAM
- **Mesalamine (5-ASA)** — PPAR-γ activation, adjunctive therapy
- **Bismuth subsalicylate** — antisecretory/anti-infective
- **Loperamide** — μ-opioid-mediated reduction in mucosal secretion/motility (symptomatic)
- **Azathioprine / methotrexate** — for budesonide-dependent/refractory patients
- **Infliximab / adalimumab** — anti-TNF, severe refractory disease
- **Vedolizumab** — anti-α4β7 integrin, gut-selective

## Clinical Relevance
- MC accounts for about 10–20% of patients with chronic diarrhoea, and diagnosis rates are highest in women aged 65 and over.
- Budesonide achieves an 80–90% short-term clinical remission rate, but 60–80% of patients relapse after discontinuation, making the quantitative design of low-dose maintenance therapy clinically important.
- This QSP model integrates drugs, bile acids, the mucosal barrier, immunity, and the collagen band to support efficacy-risk simulation of ① induction-maintenance strategies, ② bile acid sequestrant combination, and ③ vedolizumab/anti-TNF therapy.
