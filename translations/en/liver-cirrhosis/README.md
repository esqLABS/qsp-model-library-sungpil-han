## Overview
Liver cirrhosis is the final common response to chronic liver injury (viral, alcoholic, non-alcoholic fatty liver, cholestatic, and other causes), characterised by extensive fibrosis, nodule formation, and loss of normal hepatic architecture driven by hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation. Roughly 100 million people worldwide are affected, and it is a leading cause of liver-related death each year. Progression from the compensated to the decompensated stage is closely linked to portal hypertension (HVPG >10 mmHg) and leads to fatal complications such as ascites, variceal haemorrhage, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, hepatic encephalopathy, and hepatorenal syndrome. Beyond treating the underlying cause (antivirals, alcohol cessation), managing complications (non-selective beta-blockers, diuretics, rifaximin, terlipressin) is central to care.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation | TGF-β1·PDGF → differentiation into αSMA+ myofibroblasts | Collagen (I/III) accumulation, fibrosis |
| Portal hypertension | Increased intrahepatic resistance from fibrosis + splanchnic vasodilation | Elevated HVPG, varix formation |
| Intrahepatic vasoconstriction | Reduced eNOS, increased ET-1, HSC contraction | Increased intrahepatic resistance |
| Bacterial translocation | Increased gut permeability → influx of bacterial products (LPS) | Systemic inflammation, SIRS |
| Neurohormonal activation | RAAS and sympathetic nervous system activation, increased ADH secretion | Sodium and water retention, ascites |
| Declining hepatic function | Reduced albumin synthesis, coagulation factor production, and ammonia metabolism | Hypoalbuminaemia, coagulopathy, hepatic encephalopathy |
| Hepatorenal syndrome | Renal vasoconstriction, reduced GFR | AKI, chronic renal impairment |

## Drug Targets
- **Non-selective beta-blockers** (propranolol, carvedilol): reduce cardiac output plus splanchnic vasoconstriction → lower HVPG, prevent variceal haemorrhage
- **Spironolactone + furosemide** (diuretics): block aldosterone and promote urinary sodium excretion → control ascites
- **Terlipressin** (a vasopressin analogue): splanchnic vasoconstriction → treats variceal haemorrhage and hepatorenal syndrome
- **Rifaximin** (a non-absorbable antibiotic): suppresses intestinal ammonia production → prevents hepatic encephalopathy
- **Antifibrotic agents** (lobeglitazone, obeticholic acid): inhibit HSC activation; fibrosis reversal under investigation
- **TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt)**: modelling of this non-pharmacological intervention for severe ascites/variceal haemorrhage is included

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (propranolol, 3 compartments; spironolactone, 2 compartments; terlipressin, 2 compartments; rifaximin, 1 compartment) + disease PD compartments (HSC activation, fibrosis, HVPG, albumin/bilirubin/INR, ascites, aldosterone activity, GFR, creatinine, blood ammonia, hepatic encephalopathy grade)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history, ② propranolol (variceal haemorrhage prevention), ③ spironolactone + furosemide (ascites control), ④ terlipressin (hepatorenal syndrome), ⑤ rifaximin (hepatic encephalopathy prevention)
- **Calibration/rationale**: parameters based on major clinical data including the BASS trial (carvedilol), the Baveno VII consensus, and the ATTIRE trial (albumin target therapy)

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (aetiology, Child-Pugh/MELD score, presence of complications), drug PK concentration trends, key PD biomarkers (HVPG, albumin, bilirubin, INR), clinical endpoints (ascites, HE grade, survival prediction), treatment scenario comparison, and complication risk visualisation.
