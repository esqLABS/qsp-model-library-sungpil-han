# Chronic osteomyelitis · implant-associated bone infection — references

**Chronic Osteomyelitis & Implant-Associated Bone Infection — QSP model references**

Every entry in this list was **confirmed by querying PMID, title, journal and year** through the PubMed E-utilities
(no identifier here was guessed or written from memory). A ★ marks a reference on which a specific equation or
parameter of this model depends directly.

## Overview · reviews · epidemiology

1. **Osteomyelitis** (Lew DP). *Lancet*. 2004. [PMID 15276398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15276398/)
   - The classic Lancet review. The starting point for the definition, classification and treatment principles of chronic osteomyelitis.
2. **Staphylococcal Osteomyelitis: Disease Progression, Treatment Challenges, and Future Directions** (Kavanagh N). *Clin Microbiol Rev*. 2018. [PMID 29444953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29444953/)
   - A comprehensive review of staphylococcal osteomyelitis — most of this model's pathophysiological skeleton is set out here.
3. **Skeletal infections: microbial pathogenesis, immunity and clinical management** (Masters EA). *Nat Rev Microbiol*. 2022. [PMID 35169289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35169289/)
   - A recent review joining the pathogenesis, immunity and clinical management of bone infection. The basis for the sanctuary concept.
4. **Current Concepts of Osteomyelitis: From Pathologic Mechanisms to Advanced Research Methods** (Hofstee MI). *Am J Pathol*. 2020. [PMID 32194053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32194053/)
   - From mechanism to research methodology — a modern account of sequestrum, biofilm and osteocyte-lacunar infection.
5. **Chronic osteomyelitis: what the surgeon needs to know** (Panteli M). *EFORT Open Rev*. 2016. [PMID 28461939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28461939/)
   - Chronic osteomyelitis from the surgeon's point of view — the meaning of Cierny-Mader staging and of the extent of debridement.
6. **Treatment algorithms for chronic osteomyelitis** (Walter G). *Dtsch Arztebl Int*. 2012. [PMID 22536302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22536302/)
   - Treatment algorithms for chronic osteomyelitis — the division of labour between surgery and antibiotics.
7. **Trends in the epidemiology of osteomyelitis: a population-based study, 1969 to 2009** (Kremers HM). *J Bone Joint Surg Am*. 2015. [PMID 25995495](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25995495/)
   - Population-based epidemiology, 1969-2009. Osteomyelitis incidence and the diabetes-related upward trend.
8. **Prosthetic-joint infections** (Zimmerli W). *N Engl J Med*. 2004. [PMID 15483283](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15483283/)
   - The NEJM review of prosthetic joint infection — the reference text for the DAIR / one-stage / two-stage strategies.
9. **Prosthetic joint infection** (Tande AJ). *Clin Microbiol Rev*. 2014. [PMID 24696437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24696437/)
   - A comprehensive review of prosthetic joint infection (Clin Microbiol Rev).
10. **Infections associated with orthopedic implants** (Trampuz A). *Curr Opin Infect Dis*. 2006. [PMID 16804382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16804382/)
   - Orthopaedic implant infection — why a foreign body lowers the critical inoculum.
11. **Antibiotic Therapy for Prosthetic Joint Infections: An Overview** (Le Vavasseur B). *Antibiotics (Basel)*. 2022. [PMID 35453237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35453237/)
   - An overview of antibiotic therapy for prosthetic joint infection — a practical account of the drug-selection axis.
12. **General treatment principles for fracture-related infection: recommendations from an international expert group** (Metsemakers WJ). *Arch Orthop Trauma Surg*. 2020. [PMID 31659475](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31659475/)
   - General treatment principles for fracture-related infection (FRI) — an international expert consensus.
13. **Diagnosing Fracture-Related Infection: Current Concepts and Recommendations** (Govaert GAM). *J Orthop Trauma*. 2020. [PMID 31855973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31855973/)
   - Diagnostic criteria for fracture-related infection — operational definitions of confirmatory and suggestive findings.
14. **2018 International Consensus Meeting on Musculoskeletal Infection: Research Priorities from the General Assembly Questions** (Schwarz EM). *J Orthop Res*. 2019. [PMID 30977537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30977537/)
   - The 2018 International Consensus Meeting on Musculoskeletal Infection — the list of unresolved research questions.

## Foreign-body infection and the local granulocyte defect — the basis for the model's FIMBFX·AIMP50 terms

15. **Pathogenesis of foreign body infection: description and characteristics of an animal model** (Zimmerli W). *J Infect Dis*. 1982. [PMID 7119479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7119479/)
   - The tissue-cage animal model. With a foreign body present, the inoculum threshold falls dramatically.
16. **Pathogenesis of foreign body infection. Evidence for a local granulocyte defect** (Zimmerli W). *J Clin Invest*. 1984. [PMID 6323536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6323536/)
   - ★ The original paper on the local granulocyte defect. The basis for the term (AIMP50) by which implant surface area AIMP directly cuts immune access in this model.
17. **Biomaterial-centered infection: microbial adhesion versus tissue integration** (Gristina AG). *Science*. 1987. [PMID 3629258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3629258/)
   - Biomaterial-centered infection — microbial adhesion versus tissue integration.
18. **Implant infections: adhesion, biofilm formation and immune evasion** (Arciola CR). *Nat Rev Microbiol*. 2018. [PMID 29720707](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29720707/)
   - Implant infection: adhesion · biofilm formation · immune evasion (Nat Rev Microbiol).
19. **Mechanisms of Immune Evasion and Bone Tissue Colonization That Make Staphylococcus aureus the Primary Pathogen in Osteomyelitis** (Muthukrishnan G). *Curr Osteoporos Rep*. 2019. [PMID 31721069](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31721069/)
   - The immune-evasion and bone-colonisation mechanisms that make staphylococci the principal pathogen of osteomyelitis.
20. **Post-traumatic osteomyelitis: analysis of inflammatory cells recruited into the site of infection** (Wagner C). *Shock*. 2003. [PMID 14625473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14625473/)
   - Quantification of the inflammatory cells actually recruited to a post-traumatic osteomyelitis focus — functionally impaired neutrophils.

## Biofilm structure and the diffusion barrier — the basis for the spatial penalty eta = tanh(phi)/phi

21. **Diffusion in biofilms** (Stewart PS). *J Bacteriol*. 2003. [PMID 12591863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12591863/)
   - ★ The theory of diffusion within a biofilm. Reaction-diffusion equations and penetration depth — the direct basis for the Thiele-modulus form.
22. **Antimicrobial Tolerance in Biofilms** (Stewart PS). *Microbiol Spectr*. 2015. [PMID 26185072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26185072/)
   - A mechanism-by-mechanism account of antibiotic tolerance within biofilms.
23. **Antibiotic resistance of bacteria in biofilms** (Stewart PS). *Lancet*. 2001. [PMID 11463434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11463434/)
   - Antibiotic resistance of biofilm bacteria — a Lancet seminar.
24. **Contributions of antibiotic penetration, oxygen limitation, and low metabolic activity to tolerance of Pseudomonas aeruginosa biofilms to ciprofloxacin and tobramycin** (Walters MC 3rd). *Antimicrob Agents Chemother*. 2003. [PMID 12499208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12499208/)
   - ★ An experiment separating the contributions of penetration limitation, oxygen limitation and low metabolic activity. The basis for this model parameterising the spatial penalty and the phenotypic penalty separately.
25. **Spatial patterns of DNA replication, protein synthesis, and oxygen concentration within bacterial biofilms reveal diverse physiological states** (Rani SA). *J Bacteriol*. 2007. [PMID 17337582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17337582/)
   - The spatial distribution of DNA replication, protein synthesis and oxygen concentration within a biofilm — the mu gradient measured.
26. **The Calgary Biofilm Device: new technology for rapid determination of antibiotic susceptibilities of bacterial biofilms** (Ceri H). *J Clin Microbiol*. 1999. [PMID 10325322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10325322/)
   - The Calgary Biofilm Device — the original MBEC paper. The source of the observed 100-1000 times the MIC.
27. **Staphylococcal Biofilms** (Otto M). *Microbiol Spectr*. 2018. [PMID 30117414](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30117414/)
   - Staphylococcal biofilm — PIA/PNAG, the ica operon, eDNA, agr regulation.
28. **Prevention and treatment of Staphylococcus aureus biofilms** (Bhattacharya M). *Expert Rev Anti Infect Ther*. 2015. [PMID 26646248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26646248/)
   - An overview of prevention and treatment strategies for staphylococcal biofilm.
29. **Quantifying the natural history of biofilm formation in vivo during the establishment of chronic implant-associated Staphylococcus aureus osteomyelitis in mice to identify critical pathogen and host factors** (Nishitani K). *J Orthop Res*. 2015. [PMID 25820925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25820925/)
   - Quantification of the natural history of biofilm formation in implant osteomyelitis in vivo — the maturation timescale.

## Growth-rate-dependent killing and persisters — the basis for the phenotypic penalty (Emax collapse)

30. **The rate of killing of Escherichia coli by beta-lactam antibiotics is strictly proportional to the rate of bacterial growth** (Tuomanen E). *J Gen Microbiol*. 1986. [PMID 3534137](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3534137/)
   - ★ The original paper showing that the killing rate of a beta-lactam is 'strictly proportional' to growth rate. The direct basis for the KILL = Emax_gd*(mu/mu_max) term.
31. **Bactericidal effects of antibiotics on slowly growing and nongrowing bacteria** (Eng RH). *Antimicrob Agents Chemother*. 1991. [PMID 1952852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1952852/)
   - ★ A comparison of the killing activity of individual antibiotics against slow-growing and non-growing bacteria. The experiment that decides which drug has an Emax_gi.
32. **Killing of nongrowing and adherent Escherichia coli determines drug efficacy in device-related infections** (Widmer AF). *Antimicrob Agents Chemother*. 1991. [PMID 2069381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2069381/)
   - ★ Killing activity against non-growing, adherent bacteria determines drug efficacy in device-related infection.
33. **Correlation between in vivo and in vitro efficacy of antimicrobial agents against foreign body infections** (Widmer AF). *J Infect Dis*. 1990. [PMID 2355207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2355207/)
   - The correlation between in vitro and in vivo efficacy in foreign-body infection — planktonic susceptibility alone does not predict it.
34. **In vivo verification of in vitro model of antibiotic treatment of device-related infection** (Blaser J). *Antimicrob Agents Chemother*. 1995. [PMID 7625801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7625801/)
   - In vivo validation of an in vitro model of the treatment of device-related infection.
35. **Persister cells** (Lewis K). *Annu Rev Microbiol*. 2010. [PMID 20528688](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20528688/)
   - An overview of persister cells — a phenotypic state rather than genetic resistance.
36. **Small colony variants: a pathogenic form of bacteria that facilitates persistent and recurrent infections** (Proctor RA). *Nat Rev Microbiol*. 2006. [PMID 16541137](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16541137/)
   - Small-colony variants (SCV) — the pathogen form that makes persistent and relapsing infection possible.
37. **Functional relationship between bacterial cell density and the efficacy of antibiotics** (Udekwu KI). *J Antimicrob Chemother*. 2009. [PMID 19218572](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19218572/)
   - The functional relationship between bacterial density and antibiotic potency — the inoculum effect.
38. **Bacteriostatic versus bactericidal antibiotics for patients with serious bacterial infections: systematic review and meta-analysis** (Nemeth J). *J Antimicrob Chemother*. 2015. [PMID 25266070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25266070/)
   - A meta-analysis of clinical outcomes with bacteriostatic versus bactericidal antibiotics — why it matters whether Emax exceeds mu.
