## Overview
Bronchiectasis is a chronic airway disease in which the bronchial wall is irreversibly dilated and destroyed by a vicious cycle of airway infection and inflammation (Cole's vicious cycle). Worldwide prevalence is about 50~500 per 100,000 population, and the most common causes are post-pneumonia damage in childhood, primary ciliary dyskinesia, immunodeficiency, and cystic fibrosis. Airway structural damage by neutrophil elastase and chronic infection/biofilm formation by organisms such as *P. aeruginosa* are central to progression. Airway clearance therapy, inhaled antibiotics, and azithromycin maintenance therapy are the standard management approaches.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Impaired mucociliary clearance | Mucus hypersecretion, decreased MCC, CFTR abnormality | Bacterial colonisation, recurrent infection |
| Bacterial overgrowth and biofilm | *P. aeruginosa* alginate, fluorescent biofilm | Antibiotic resistance, chronic infection |
| Neutrophil-driven inflammation | IL-8 → neutrophil recruitment → secretion of NE, MMP | Destruction of airway wall structure |
| Neutrophil elastase (NE) | Degradation of elastin/collagen, inactivation of SLPI | Irreversible bronchial dilation |
| Vicious cycle amplification | Bacteria → more neutrophils → more NE | Progressive decline in pulmonary function |
| Acute exacerbation | Surge in bacterial load, cytokine storm | Hospitalisation, accelerated FEV1 loss |

## Drug Targets
- **Azithromycin (long-term low-dose)**: antibacterial + anti-inflammatory (↓IL-8, ↓neutrophil migration) — reduces exacerbation frequency (EMBRACE trial)
- **Inhaled tobramycin (TIP)**: high pulmonary concentration antibacterial → suppresses *P. aeruginosa* — based on the RESPIRE trial
- **Ciprofloxacin (oral)**: systemic antibacterial during acute exacerbation — 14-day regimen
- **Dornase alfa (inhaled DNase)**: degrades viscous DNA → decreases mucus viscosity, improves MCC — mainly cystic fibrosis
- **Inhaled hypertonic saline/mannitol**: airway hydration → improves MCC — non-cystic fibrosis BEX

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK (azithromycin 3 compartments, inhaled tobramycin 2 compartments, oral ciprofloxacin 2 compartments, dornase alfa lung compartment), disease state (bacterial load · biofilm · neutrophils · IL-8 · NE · MCC · airway damage · exacerbation status)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated (natural history), ② azithromycin 250 mg 3×/week maintenance (EMBRACE), ③ inhaled tobramycin 300 mg BID cycling (RESPIRE), ④ AZM+inhaled tobramycin combination, ⑤ AZM+TIP+dornase alfa triple therapy, ⑥ ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID×14 days during acute exacerbation
- **Calibration/rationale**: referenced against data from EMBRACE (azithromycin), RESPIRE-1/2 (inhaled ciprofloxacin), and the Chalmers exacerbation prediction model

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile (underlying disease, bacterial culture, baseline FEV1, exacerbation frequency), pharmacokinetics (plasma/pulmonary concentrations of each antibiotic), an infection PD tab (bacterial load, biofilm, NE), an airway inflammation/damage tab (IL-8, neutrophils, MCC, airway damage score), comparison of 6 treatment scenarios, and an exacerbation prediction biomarker tab.

## Usage
```r
library(mrgsolve)
mod <- mread("bex_mrgsolve_model.R")
out <- mrgsim(mod, end = 365)
plot(out)
```
