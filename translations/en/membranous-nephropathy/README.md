## Overview
Membranous nephropathy (MN) is one of the most common causes of primary
nephrotic syndrome in adults, accounting for roughly 20-37% of
glomerulonephritis worldwide. In roughly 70-80% of primary MN, autoantibodies
(anti-PLA2R antibodies) are found against the M-type phospholipase A2
receptor (PLA2R1), a podocyte surface antigen. The core mechanism is that
anti-PLA2R antibody-antigen immune complexes deposit subepithelially on the
glomerular basement membrane, forming the complement membrane attack complex
(MAC), which damages podocytes, thickens the glomerular basement membrane,
and produces massive proteinuria (nephrotic syndrome). B-cell depletion with
rituximab (anti-CD20) has now become the standard treatment for primary MN,
alongside calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, cyclosporine) and
cyclophosphamide (the Ponticelli regimen).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways
| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Anti-PLA2R antibody generation | B cells/plasma cells generate anti-PLA2R1 IgG4 (HLA-DQA1-linked) | Positive serum anti-PLA2R antibody |
| Subepithelial immune complex deposition | Anti-PLA2R IgG binds PLA2R1 on the podocyte surface -> subepithelial IC formation | GBM thickening, spike formation |
| Complement activation | Lectin/classical pathway -> generation of C3, C5b-9 (MAC) | Direct podocyte injury |
| Podocyte injury | MAC-mediated oxidative stress, actin reorganisation, loss of slit-diaphragm proteins | Increased proteinuria |
| Glomerular basement membrane changes | Podocyte injury -> abnormal GBM remodelling | GBM thickening, subepithelial deposits |
| Albumin loss | Massive proteinuria -> reduced serum albumin | Oedema, thrombosis risk |
| Renin-angiotensin activation | Hypoalbuminaemia -> RAAS activation -> water/sodium retention | Worsening oedema, declining renal function |

## Key Drug Targets
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): B-cell depletion -> suppresses anti-PLA2R antibody generation, efficacy demonstrated in the GEMRITUX and MENTOR trials
- **Tacrolimus** (calcineurin inhibitor): suppresses T-cell-dependent B-cell activity, direct podocyte protection
- **Cyclophosphamide** (alkylating agent): Ponticelli regimen (alternating cyclophosphamide and steroids), for severe MN
- **ACEi/ARB**: reduce proteinuria and relieve glomerular hypertension, first-line conservative therapy for all patients
- **Ofatumumab / obinutuzumab** (next-generation anti-CD20): alternatives for rituximab-refractory disease
- **Belimumab** (anti-BAFF): under investigation for suppressing plasma-cell survival signalling

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (rituximab 2-compartment +
  CD20 binding, tacrolimus 2-compartment, cyclophosphamide 2-compartment +
  metabolite) + disease PD compartments (CD20 B cells/plasma cells,
  anti-PLA2R1 antibody, IgG deposition, complement MAC, podocyte injury, GBM
  thickening, proteinuria, serum albumin, eGFR, angiotensin II, aldosterone)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history (including the possibility
  of spontaneous remission), ② rituximab alone (375 mg/m² x 1-2 doses), ③
  tacrolimus + ACEi, ④ Ponticelli regimen (cyclophosphamide + steroids), ⑤
  repeat rituximab dosing (on B-cell repopulation), ⑥ tacrolimus + rituximab
  combination
- **Calibration/basis**: parameters set from data from the MENTOR trial
  (rituximab vs. cyclosporine), the GEMRITUX trial, and the RI-CYCLO trial

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (baseline anti-PLA2R antibody
titre, proteinuria, eGFR, serum albumin), drug PK concentration trends
(rituximab, tacrolimus), key PD biomarkers (CD20 B cells, anti-PLA2R
antibody, complement), clinical endpoints (complete/partial remission of
proteinuria, change in eGFR), treatment scenario comparison, and predicted
response by immunosuppression intensity.
