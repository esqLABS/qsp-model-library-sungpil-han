## Overview

Goodpasture syndrome (anti-GBM disease) is a rare autoimmune disease in which autoantibodies against the NC1 domain of the type IV collagen α3 chain bind the glomerular basement membrane (GBM) and alveolar basement membrane, triggering complement activation and neutrophil infiltration. Annual incidence is very rare, at about 0.5~1 per million, and it is characterised by pulmonary-renal syndrome, in which rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis and alveolar haemorrhage occur simultaneously. The HLA-DR15 allele is the main genetic risk factor, and smoking and exposure to hydrocarbon solvents are known triggers of pulmonary haemorrhage. Standard treatment is plasmapheresis to remove circulating antibodies combined with immunosuppression using cyclophosphamide (CY) and high-dose corticosteroids, with rituximab used in refractory or relapsing cases.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Anti-GBM antibody formation | HLA-DR15-restricted antigen presentation → B cell activation → plasma cells → IgG anti-α3(IV) antibody | Linear glomerular IgG deposition |
| Complement activation | Antibody-GBM binding → classical pathway C3 activation → generation of C5a/MAC | Neutrophil recruitment, endothelial damage |
| Neutrophil infiltration | C5a receptor stimulation → oxidative burst → protease secretion | Glomerular crescent formation, decreased GFR |
| Th1 immune response | CD4 T cells → IFN-γ · TNF-α → macrophage activation | Sustained inflammation and tissue destruction |
| Alveolar damage | Smoking/inhalational exposure → exposure of alveolar basement membrane antigen → antibody binding | Alveolar haemorrhage, haemoptysis |
| Regulatory T cell deficiency | Decreased Treg function → breakdown of self-tolerance | Sustained autoimmunity |

## Drug Targets

- **Plasmapheresis**: direct removal of circulating anti-GBM antibody; prompt initial treatment
- **Cyclophosphamide (CY)**: alkylation-mediated suppression of B cells/plasma cells; decreased antibody production
- **High-dose corticosteroids (prednisolone)**: suppresses inflammation, blocks neutrophil migration, mitigates downstream complement effects
- **Rituximab (RTX)**: anti-CD20 → B cell depletion; used in relapsing/refractory anti-GBM disease
- **Avacopan**: C5aR1 blocker; expected to reduce nephrotoxicity when ANCA vasculitis coexists
- **Immunoadsorption**: selective removal of anti-GBM IgG using protein A/G columns

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: includes drug PK (CY/active metabolite, prednisolone, RTX 2 compartments, avacopan) + disease PD (anti-GBM antibody, B cells, plasma cells, C5a, renal neutrophils, GBM damage, GFR, lung damage, DLCO, proteinuria, haematuria, CRP, Treg)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated baseline, ② plasmapheresis+CY+steroid (standard), ③ RTX alone, ④ plasmapheresis+RTX+steroid, ⑤ avacopan combination, ⑥ early vs. delayed treatment comparison
- **Calibration/rationale**: qualitatively calibrated the GFR recovery curve and antibody titre decline kinetics based on anti-GBM disease cohort data from Levy et al. (Ann Intern Med 2001), McAdoo & Pusey (Clin J Am Soc Nephrol 2017), and others

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of tabs for patient profile (initial GFR, anti-GBM titre, presence of pulmonary involvement), drug PK simulation (RTX/CY plasma concentrations), key PD markers (antibody titre, complement C5a, neutrophils), renal clinical endpoints (GFR trend, proteinuria, haematuria), pulmonary clinical endpoints (DLCO, pulmonary haemorrhage markers), comparison of 6 treatment scenarios, and biomarkers.
