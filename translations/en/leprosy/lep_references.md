## The quantitative anchors this model took from the literature

| Anchor | Value | Supporting section |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Generation time of M. leprae | 12.5 days (the longest known) | §2 |
| A single rifampicin 600 mg dose → kill of viable organisms | ~3 log10 (99.9%) | §8 |
| Time for the morphological index (MI) to reach 0 | 2–3 weeks on rifampicin / ~5 months on dapsone | §6, §8 |
| Rate of fall of the bacterial index (BI) | 0.6–1.0 log10/year (on every effective regimen) | §6, §7 |
| Half-life for degradation of dead organisms (model estimate) | ~137 days | §6 |
| Terminal half-life of clofazimine | ~70 days | §10 |
| Dapsone 100 mg/day → methaemoglobin | 5–12% | §9 |
| Relapse after 12 months of MDT-MB | 0.7–3% at 10 years | §7, §11 |
| Single-dose rifampicin prophylaxis for contacts | ~57% reduction in 2-year risk | §15 |
| 20 weeks versus 32 weeks of prednisolone | the longer course gives better nerve function outcomes | §13 |

## The parts of this model not validated against the literature (explicitly unverified)

1. **Disinhibition of the degradation rate of dead organisms (`CLRBOOST = 2.0`)** — the assumption
   that live organisms suppress phagosome maturation and that degradation speeds up once they die.
   Not a value measured directly in any single experiment, but back-calculated from the difference in BI decline rate on and before treatment.
2. **The prediction that clofazimine loading (300 mg/day for one month) cuts the cumulative ENL
   burden by 49%** — the most exposed claim in this model; no clinical trial has ever made this comparison.
3. **The containment threshold `SPEC* = 0.0225`** — the minimum immune setting at which the host
   can suppress a residual bacterial clone. Derived inside the model; its clinical counterpart has never been measured directly.
4. **The nerve damage index (0–100)** — calibrated to the shape of the WHO disability grades, not
   fitted to individual nerve conduction studies.
