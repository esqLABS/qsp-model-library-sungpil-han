## The five behaviours this model was designed to reproduce

1. **Normalising serum calcium does not normalise urinary calcium.** Conventional treatment only
   raises the filtered load and cannot raise the reabsorbed fraction, so hypercalciuria follows the
   moment the calcium target is reached (scenario 3 vs 8). [4,11,18]
2. **Only PTH replacement lowers urinary calcium at the same serum calcium.** Because it restores
   the renal axis (scenarios 8, 9 and 15). [89,92]
3. **Pulsatile and flat exposure act differently on bone.** The osteoclast follows the time-averaged
   PTH and the osteoblast a memory of the peak, so teriparatide is anabolic while TransCon comes
   closer to normalising turnover (scenario 7 vs 8). [77,78,91]
4. **In ADH1 calcium and calcitriol are especially dangerous.** The renal tubular CaSR is
   simultaneously overactive, so urinary calcium is higher at the same serum calcium. Encaleret
   moves the set-point back and produces serum calcium↑ together with urinary calcium↓ (scenario 10 vs 11). [28,33,95]
5. **Hypomagnesaemia blocks secretion and action at the same time.** Hence it is refractory to
   calcium and calcitriol and recovers abruptly with magnesium repletion (scenario 12). [3,7]
