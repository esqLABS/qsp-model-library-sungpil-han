## Overview

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disease of the neuromuscular junction (NMJ),
mediated by anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) antibodies (about 85%) or by
anti-MuSK/anti-LRP4 antibodies. Worldwide prevalence is about 20 per 100,000, with
early onset common in women and younger age groups (20s–30s) and a second peak in men
over 60. The antibodies either directly block AChR or form the complement-mediated
membrane attack complex (MAC), damaging the NMJ, which characteristically produces
fluctuating muscle weakness and fatigue. In the thymus (thymoma or thymic
hyperplasia), autoreactive T follicular helper cells (TFH) activate GC B cells to
produce the antibodies.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| TFH-GC B cell axis | Thymic TFH → GC B cells → short- and long-lived plasma cells | Sustained production of anti-AChR IgG |
| Direct AChR antibody blockade | IgG1/IgG3 → AChR binding and cross-linking | Reduced postsynaptic AChR density |
| Complement activation | C3b opsonization → MAC (C5b-9) formation | NMJ structural destruction, muscle fiber damage |
| FcRn-mediated IgG recycling | FcRn rescues IgG from degradation → extends antibody half-life | Sustained high antibody titer |
| Cholinesterase inhibition | ACh accumulation → more AChR activation | Temporary improvement in muscle strength |
| Thymic lesions | Thymoma (10–15%) or thymic hyperplasia | Worsening immune dysregulation |
| Reduced NMJ safety factor | Decreased MEPP → nerve-muscle transmission failure | Increased QMG score, reduced respiratory muscle strength |

## Key Drug Targets

- **Pyridostigmine**: inhibits acetylcholinesterase (AChE) → ACh accumulation, symptom relief
- **Prednisolone**: broad immunosuppression; suppresses TFH/B-cell activity, expands Treg
- **Azathioprine**: blocks purine synthesis via conversion to 6-MP/6-TGN; long-term maintenance therapy
- **Eculizumab**: inhibits C5 complement → blocks MAC formation (REGAIN trial)
- **Efgartigimod**: blocks FcRn → rapidly reduces blood IgG levels (including anti-AChR antibodies)
- **Rituximab**: anti-CD20 B-cell depletion; effective in anti-MuSK MG and refractory MG

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: pyridostigmine oral 2-compartment PK, prednisolone 2-compartment, azathioprine (GUT→6-MP→6-TGN) 3-compartment, eculizumab 2-compartment + C5 binding, efgartigimod 1-compartment + FcRn, thymic TFH · GC B-cell · SLPC · LLPC cell compartments, anti-AChR IgG, AChR density, complement activation, NMJ safety factor and QMG score
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated MG ② pyridostigmine alone ③ pyridostigmine+prednisolone ④ pyridostigmine+prednisolone+azathioprine ⑤ eculizumab+pyridostigmine ⑥ efgartigimod+pyridostigmine ⑦ rituximab+pyridostigmine
- **Calibration/evidence**: calibrated against clinical trial data from REGAIN (eculizumab), ADAPT (efgartigimod), and MGTX (thymectomy)

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① **Patient Profile** (antibody type, baseline QMG, thymic status settings), ② **PK** (plasma drug concentrations, FcRn occupancy, eculizumab C5 inhibition rate), ③ **Key PD Metrics** (anti-AChR IgG, AChR density, complement activation trends), ④ **Clinical Endpoints** (QMG, MGC, NMJ safety factor), ⑤ **Scenario Comparison** (direct comparison of 7 treatment strategies), ⑥ **Biomarkers** (IgG reduction rate, SLPC/LLPC changes).
