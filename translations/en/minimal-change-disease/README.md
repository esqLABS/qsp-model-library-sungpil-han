## Overview
Minimal change disease (MCD) is a glomerular disease characterised by normal findings on light microscopy but loss of podocyte foot processes on electron microscopy, accounting for about 70~80% of paediatric nephrotic syndrome and about 15~25% of adult cases. In children in particular, annual incidence is about 2~7 per 100,000. The core pathogenesis is understood as a circulating permeability factor, secreted due to T cell immunoregulatory abnormality, damaging slit diaphragm proteins such as nephrin and podocin in podocytes to cause massive proteinuria; recently anti-nephrin antibodies have also been found in some patients, drawing attention to a role for B cells as well. Most patients respond to steroids (prednisolone), but about 50% relapse, and calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, cyclosporine) or rituximab are used for steroid-dependent or frequently relapsing disease.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| T cell immunoregulatory abnormality | CD4+ Th2 predominance · decreased Treg, excess IL-13 · IL-4 production | Release of circulating permeability factor |
| Circulating permeability factor | Identity undetermined (hemopexin · CLCF-1 · CD80?), induces podocyte damage | Slit diaphragm protein damage, foot process effacement |
| Anti-nephrin antibody | IgG anti-nephrin autoantibody (some patients) → direct slit diaphragm damage | Acute massive proteinuria |
| Podocyte slit diaphragm damage | Rearrangement/loss of nephrin · podocin · synaptopodin | Selective proteinuria (albumin > IgG) |
| Hypoalbuminaemia | Massive albumin loss → serum albumin <2.5 g/dL | Oedema, thrombosis, hypocalcaemia |
| Secondary hyperlipidaemia | Increased LDL production, HDL loss → elevated total cholesterol | Cardiovascular risk, lipiduria |
| Tubular damage | Tubular toxicity from protein overload | Rarely accompanied by ATN |

## Drug Targets
- **Prednisolone** (high-dose steroid): suppresses T cells/B cells, blocks NF-κB → inhibits production of the permeability factor (first-line, 2 mg/kg/day in children)
- **Tacrolimus** (calcineurin inhibitor): IL-2 inhibition → blocks T cell activation, direct podocyte protective effect; for steroid dependence/frequent relapse
- **Cyclosporine** (calcineurin inhibitor): mechanism similar to tacrolimus, second-line choice
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): modulates B cells/T cells, increasingly used for frequently relapsing/steroid-dependent MCD
- **Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)**: for steroid-sparing purposes, in steroid-dependent adults
- **Cyclophosphamide**: for long-term remission induction, historically used in frequently relapsing children

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (prednisolone 3 compartments, cyclosporine 2 compartments, tacrolimus 2 compartments, rituximab 2 compartments) + disease PD compartments (CD4 effector T cells, Treg, B cells, permeability factor, anti-nephrin antibody, podocyte damage, slit diaphragm damage, proteinuria, serum albumin, oedema, cholesterol, eGFR)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history (untreated), ② high-dose prednisolone induction, ③ prednisolone + cyclosporine (frequent relapse), ④ prednisolone + tacrolimus, ⑤ rituximab + low-dose steroid, ⑥ MMF + low-dose steroid (steroid-sparing)
- **Calibration/rationale**: parameters set based on the KDIGO 2021 nephrotic syndrome guidelines, the MENTOR MCD cohort, and ICON paediatric nephrotic syndrome study data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (age · baseline proteinuria · serum albumin · relapse history), drug PK concentration trends (steroids · calcineurin inhibitors · rituximab), key PD biomarkers (podocyte damage · CD20 B cells · T cells), clinical endpoints (proteinuria remission · relapse count · change in eGFR), treatment scenario comparison, and prediction of relapse risk and cumulative steroid dose.
