## Overview
Celiac disease is a systemic autoimmune disease in which, in individuals with an
HLA-DQ2/DQ8-positive genetic predisposition, gluten (gliadin/glutenin from wheat,
barley, and rye) triggers an autoimmune response in the small-intestinal mucosa,
causing villous atrophy and malabsorption. Prevalence in the West reaches about 1% of
the population (the diagnosed rate is much lower), and deamidated gluten peptides
(DGP) produced by tissue transglutaminase (tTG) are presented on HLA-DQ, triggering a
CD4 Th1/Th17 response. A completely gluten-free diet (GFD) is the only approved
treatment, and new drugs are in development, including a barrier-permeability blocker
(larazotide), a tTG2 inhibitor (ZED1227), and an anti-IL-15 antibody (AMG714).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways
| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Increased intestinal permeability | Zonulin upregulation, tight-junction damage → gliadin crosses the barrier | Gluten influx into the lamina propria |
| tTG2 deamidation | tTG2 → gliadin Gln→Glu → maximized antigenicity | Increased HLA-DQ2/8 binding efficiency |
| HLA-DQ2/8 presentation | DC → CD4+ Th1/Th17 activation | IFN-γ/IL-17-driven mucosal damage |
| IL-15-mediated IEL activation | IL-15 → NK-T-like IEL → direct epithelial cytotoxicity | Destruction of the villous epithelium |
| B-cell/autoantibody production | tTG2 IgA autoantibodies, anti-DGP IgA/IgG | Diagnostic biomarkers, additional tissue damage |
| Nutrient malabsorption | Villous atrophy → reduced absorptive surface area | Iron, folate, calcium, vitamin D deficiency |

## Key Drug Targets
- **Gluten-free diet (GFD)**: removes the antigen → immune response resolves — the only standard treatment
- **Larazotide acetate (barrier protectant)**: strengthens tight junctions → reduces intestinal permeability — buffers gluten exposure
- **ZED1227 (tTG2 inhibitor)**: competitively inhibits tTG2 → blocks DGP generation — improved villous atrophy in phase 2 trials
- **AMG714 (anti-IL-15)**: neutralizes IL-15 → suppresses IEL activation — targeted at refractory celiac disease (RCD)

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: antigen pathway (intestinal gluten, lamina propria DGP, tTG2 activation, intestinal permeability), immunity (IEL, CD4 T cells, IFN-γ, IL-17, IL-21, B cells, anti-tTG IgA), tissue pathology (villous height VH, villous:crypt ratio CrD), absorptive function (absorptive area, iron stores, BMD), drug PK (DrugGut→DrugPlasma)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① no treatment (normal diet), ② full GFD, ③ partial GFD (5% contamination), ④ GFD+larazotide 2 mg TID, ⑤ GFD+ZED1227 (tTG2 inhibitor), ⑥ GFD+AMG714 (anti-IL-15, RCD)
- **Calibration/evidence**: calibrated against the ZED1227 phase 2 trial (Tye-Din et al.), Kelly's villous-atrophy recovery data, and Fernández-Bañares antibody-titer data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile (HLA subtype, gluten intake, anti-tTG IgA at
diagnosis), pharmacokinetics (plasma concentrations of drug candidates), immune
response (IEL, CD4 T cells, antibody titer), tissue pathology (villous height, V:C
ratio), treatment scenario comparison (6 scenarios), and nutritional/long-term
prognosis biomarkers (iron, BMD, absorptive area).

## Usage
```r
library(mrgsolve)
mod <- mread("cd_mrgsolve_model.R")
out <- mrgsim(mod, end = 365)
plot(out)
# Shiny dashboard:
shiny::runApp("cd_shiny_app.R")
```
