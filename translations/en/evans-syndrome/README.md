## Overview

Evans syndrome (ES) is a rare, multi-lineage autoimmune cytopenia in which autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA) and immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) occur simultaneously or sequentially. Its prevalence is extremely rare, at about 0.02 per 100,000 population, and it occurs in both children and adults. The core pathophysiology comprises generation of autoreactive B cells producing anti-erythrocyte (warm IgG-type) and anti-platelet autoantibodies, antibody-mediated cytopenia via splenic destruction, and defective Treg function. Treatment proceeds in a stepwise manner using steroids, rituximab, IVIG, and thrombopoiesis-stimulating agents (TPO-RAs).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecule/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Autoreactive B cells | Proliferation of B-cell clones reactive to autoantigens | Generation of anti-RBC and anti-platelet autoantibodies |
| Treg deficiency | Reduced FoxP3+ Treg number/function → impaired peripheral tolerance | Sustained autoimmune response |
| Fc-receptor-mediated cytopenia | IgG-coated erythrocytes/platelets → splenic macrophage phagocytosis | Haemolysis, thrombocytopenia |
| Complement-mediated haemolysis | Anti-IgM (cold type) → complement → intravascular haemolysis | Acute anaemia, haemoglobinuria |
| Suppression of platelet production | Autoantibodies/T cells → inhibited megakaryocyte maturation | Worsened thrombocytopenia |
| PI3K-δ pathway | (in some ALPS-associated cases) → resistance to lymphocyte apoptosis | Accumulation of autoimmune lymphocytes |
| Secondary immunodeficiency | Complication of immunosuppressive treatment | Increased infection risk |

## Drug Targets

- **Corticosteroids** (prednisolone): suppress autoantibody production, inhibit splenic phagocytosis; first-line therapy
- **Rituximab**: anti-CD20 → B-cell depletion → reduced autoantibodies; second-line therapy
- **IVIG**: Fc-receptor blockade → blocks antibody-mediated cytopenia; controls the acute phase
- **TPO receptor agonists** (eltrombopag, romiplostim): stimulate megakaryocytes → promote platelet production
- **Sirolimus** (mTOR inhibitor): suppresses autoreactive lymphocytes in ALPS-associated Evans syndrome
- **Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)**: inhibits lymphocyte proliferation; maintenance therapy for steroid tapering

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (prednisolone, MPA, eltrombopag, sirolimus, IVIG, each with depot/central) + PD compartments (autoreactive B cells, plasma cells, autoantibodies [anti-RBC, anti-platelet], Treg/Teff ratio, red-cell count, haemoglobin, platelet count, megakaryocytes, splenic phagocytosis index, complement activation)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① no treatment (natural course), ② corticosteroid alone (first-line), ③ rituximab + prednisolone (second-line), ④ IVIG (acute-phase rescue), ⑤ sirolimus + steroid (refractory, ALPS-associated)
- **Calibration/rationale**: based on the Michel et al. Evans syndrome cohort, Aladjidi paediatric AIHA data, and meta-analyses of rituximab in AIHA/ITP

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① Patient Profile (baseline haemoglobin, platelets, Coombs test, Treg level settings), ② PK tab (plasma concentrations of steroids, rituximab, IVIG, TPO-RA), ③ Immune PD tab (B-cell, autoantibody, Treg/Teff ratio, complement trends), ④ Clinical Endpoints (haemoglobin, platelets, Hb remission attainment rate), ⑤ Scenario Comparison (simultaneous comparison of the 5 treatment strategies), ⑥ Biomarkers (plasma-cell, megakaryocyte, splenic phagocytosis index trends).
