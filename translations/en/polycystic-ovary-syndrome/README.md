## Overview
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is the most common endocrine disease, affecting 5~15% of reproductive-age women, and is characterised by hyperandrogenism, chronic anovulation, and polycystic ovarian morphology (per the Rotterdam 2003 criteria). The core pathogenic mechanism is a mutually amplifying vicious cycle of ① increased hypothalamic GnRH pulse frequency (abnormality of kisspeptin-neurokinin B-dynorphin [KNDy] neurons) → excess LH/relatively decreased FSH, ② CYP17A1 hyperactivity in ovarian theca cells → androgen overproduction, and ③ insulin resistance → hyperinsulinaemia → decreased SHBG → increased free androgen. The main treatment targets are HPO axis regulation, improved insulin sensitivity, and suppression of androgen excess.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| GnRH pulse pathway | KNDy neuron abnormality, increased GnRH pulse frequency | Increased LH/FSH ratio (>2) |
| Ovarian steroid pathway | CYP17A1 hyperactivity, LH overstimulation | Excess androstenedione/testosterone |
| Insulin resistance pathway | Post-INSR signalling abnormality (PI3K-Akt), IRS-1 phosphorylation | Hyperinsulinaemia · compensatory hyperinsulinism |
| SHBG-free androgen pathway | Decreased hepatic SHBG synthesis (insulin suppression) | Increased bioavailable testosterone |
| Follicular maturation disorder pathway | Excess AMH, insufficient FSH, follicular arrest | Polycystic ovaries · anovulation · infertility |
| Metabolic complication pathway | Visceral fat accumulation, dyslipidaemia, inflammation | Increased risk of type 2 diabetes/cardiovascular disease |

## Drug Targets
- **Metformin**: AMPK activation → improves insulin sensitivity, suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis, indirectly suppresses ovarian androgen production
- **Clomiphene/letrozole**: oestrogen receptor antagonism / aromatase inhibition → increased FSH → ovulation induction
- **Oral contraceptive pills (OCP)**: ethinylestradiol+progestin → LH suppression, increased SHBG, decreased androgens
- **Spironolactone/cyproterone**: androgen receptor blockade → improves hirsutism/acne
- **GnRH analogues**: downregulation of the HPO axis — prevents overstimulation during IVF

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: PD compartments for GnRH drive · LH · FSH · total testosterone · oestradiol · progesterone · AMH · AFC status · dominant follicle · insulin · blood glucose · SHBG · free testosterone · IGFBP1 · CRP · BMI · hirsutism score, and PK compartments for metformin · letrozole · ethinylestradiol · spironolactone · clomiphene
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① S1 untreated PCOS, ② S2 metformin, ③ S3 letrozole, ④ S4 oral contraceptive pill, ⑤ S5 metformin+letrozole, ⑥ S6 spironolactone
- **Calibration/rationale**: calibrated with reference to ovulation rate and hormone-change data from the standard PCOS clinical trial (NICHD PPCOSII) and randomised comparative studies of metformin/letrozole

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile (Rotterdam phenotype, BMI, insulin resistance index), HPO axis hormone PK/PD, follicular maturation/ovulation PD, metabolic marker clinical endpoints (HOMA-IR, blood glucose, lipids), treatment scenario comparison (ovulation rate/androgen change), and biomarkers (AMH, SHBG, free testosterone)

## Usage
```r

out <- mrgsim(mod, end = 365)
plot(out)
```
