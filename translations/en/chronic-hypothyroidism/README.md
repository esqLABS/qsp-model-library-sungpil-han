## Overview

Chronic hypothyroidism is a common endocrine disease affecting about 5% of adult women and about 1–2% of men, with Hashimoto thyroiditis as its most common cause. Feedback regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis breaks down, characteristically producing elevated TSH and reduced FT4, together with combined systemic effects such as decreased metabolic rate, reduced heart rate, elevated LDL, and abnormal bone mineral density. Standard treatment is supplementation with levothyroxine (LT4) alone or LT4 combined with liothyronine (LT3), with dose titration as the central concern.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Disrupted HPT axis feedback | Reduced T4/T3 → increased TRH → increased TSH | Excess thyroid stimulation, goitre |
| Peripheral T4→T3 conversion | Reduced 5'-deiodinase (DIO1/DIO2) activity | Insufficient active T3 in tissue |
| Reduced metabolism | Decreased basal metabolic rate (BMR), reduced oxygen consumption | Weight gain, fatigue, hypothermia |
| Cardiovascular effects | Reduced heart rate and cardiac output, decreased myocardial contractility | Bradycardia, pericardial effusion |
| Abnormal lipid metabolism | Reduced LDL receptor → elevated LDL | Increased cardiovascular risk |
| Abnormal bone metabolism | Reduced bone remodelling rate → changes in BMD | Reduced BMD with long-term over-treatment causing hyperthyroidism |
| Neuromuscular effects | Reduced Na-K-ATPase activity, slowed nerve conduction velocity | Cognitive impairment, muscle cramps |

## Drug Targets

- **Levothyroxine (LT4)**: T4 supplementation, exerting its effect via peripheral T4→T3 conversion; long half-life (7 days) allows once-daily dosing
- **Liothyronine (LT3)**: direct supplementation of active T3, with faster tissue response but a short half-life (1 day) → requires divided dosing when combined
- **Deiodinase modulation**: supplementary nutrients such as selenium support DIO1/DIO2 activity (adjunctive therapy)
- **TSH receptor**: the key target of LT4 dose-titration feedback — over-suppression risks bone loss and atrial fibrillation

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: endogenous hormone dynamics for TRH, TSH, total T4 (TT4), total T3 (TT3), and rT3 + LT4/LT3 gut and central (2-compartment) PK + PD compartments for heart rate (HR), LDL, basal metabolic rate (BMR), symptom score, and bone mineral density (BMD)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: (1) euthyroid, (2) untreated hypothyroidism, (3) LT4 100 μg QD, (4) partial thyroid remnant + LT4 50 μg, (5) LT4 175 μg (over-treatment), (6) LT4 100 μg + LT3 10 μg combination, (7) LT4 125 μg high dose
- **Calibration/rationale**: based on the Jonklaas J et al. (Thyroid 2014) recommendations and Hoermann et al. data on the non-linear TSH-FT4 relationship

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 7 tabs: (1) patient profile — setting residual thyroid function, weight, and age; (2) PK tab — visualising LT4/LT3 serum concentrations and half-lives; (3) HPT axis — time series of TRH, TSH, FT4, FT3; (4) key PD metrics — HR, BMR, LDL; (5) clinical endpoints — symptom score, BMD; (6) scenario comparison — comparing 7 dosing strategies; (7) biomarkers — TSH target-range attainment, T3/T4 ratio
