## Overview
Microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) is a small-vessel necrotising vasculitis characterised by positivity for MPO-ANCA (anti-myeloperoxidase antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody). Annual incidence is rare, at about 3~7 per million, but delayed diagnosis leads to life-threatening conditions such as rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (RPGN) and pulmonary capillaritis (pulmonary haemorrhage). MPO-ANCA directly stimulates over-activated neutrophils, causing them to adhere to endothelial cells and release an oxidative burst and cytotoxic enzymes, thereby producing necrotising vasculitis. The alternative complement pathway (the C5a-C5aR1 axis) also amplifies neutrophil activation. Treatment is divided into induction (rituximab or cyclophosphamide + steroids) and maintenance (rituximab or azathioprine) phases, and avacopan (a C5aR1 inhibitor) was approved in 2021 as a steroid-sparing strategy.

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| MPO-ANCA production | Abnormal B cell activation, increased CD4 Th17, decreased Treg | Positive serum MPO-ANCA |
| Neutrophil hyperactivation | MPO-ANCA stimulates FcγRIIa · PR3 on primed neutrophils | Oxidative burst, NET formation, endothelial damage |
| Complement C5a-C5aR1 axis | Alternative complement pathway activation → C5a generation → stimulation of neutrophil C5aR1 | Amplifying feedback of neutrophil recruitment/activation |
| Renal necrotising glomerulonephritis | Glomerular capillary endothelial damage, crescent formation | Rapidly progressive renal failure |
| Pulmonary capillaritis | Pulmonary capillary endothelial damage, erythrocyte extravasation | Alveolar haemorrhage, DAH |
| Systemic inflammation | Elevated CRP · ESR, fever, weight loss | Reflected in BVAS score |
| Tissue fibrosis | Fibrotic conversion of crescents, TGF-β-mediated | Chronic decline in renal function |

## Drug Targets
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): B cell depletion → suppresses MPO-ANCA production; induction/maintenance efficacy demonstrated in the RAVE/RITUXVAS trials
- **Cyclophosphamide** (alkylating agent): B/T cell depletion, induction therapy for severe pulmonary/renal involvement
- **Prednisolone** (high-dose steroid): suppresses acute inflammation, combined during the induction period
- **Avacopan** (C5aR1 inhibitor): blocks the complement C5a-C5aR1 axis → suppresses neutrophil activation, steroid-sparing; non-inferiority for complete remission demonstrated in the ADVOCATE trial
- **Azathioprine / methotrexate**: maintenance immunosuppression after remission
- **Belimumab + rituximab**: BAFF-blockade combination strategy under investigation

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (cyclophosphamide 3 compartments + 4-OH-CY metabolite, rituximab 2 compartments, prednisolone 2 compartments, avacopan 2 compartments) + disease PD compartments (B cells, plasma cells, MPO-ANCA, C5a, activated neutrophils, endothelial damage, renal inflammation/fibrosis, GFR, pulmonary inflammation, CRP)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history, ② cyclophosphamide + steroid (induction), ③ rituximab + steroid (induction), ④ avacopan + rituximab (steroid-sparing), ⑤ maintenance: rituximab every 6 months
- **Calibration/rationale**: parameters set based on data from the RAVE (rituximab vs CY), ADVOCATE (avacopan), and MEPEX (plasma exchange) trials

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (MPO-ANCA titre · baseline eGFR · BVAS · presence of pulmonary involvement), drug PK concentration trends, key PD biomarkers (B cells · MPO-ANCA · C5a · CRP), clinical endpoints (BVAS · eGFR · change in proteinuria), treatment scenario comparison, and prediction of remission maintenance and relapse risk.
