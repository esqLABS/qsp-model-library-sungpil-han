## Overview

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a disease in which repetitive collapse of the upper airway during sleep produces apnoea (≥10 seconds) and hypopnoea, with an adult prevalence of about 10–30% (about 4–7% at moderate-to-severe or greater). Obesity, structural upper airway abnormalities (small mandible, tonsillar hypertrophy), and reduced upper airway dilator muscle tone are the key risk factors. Intermittent hypoxia (IH) increases the risk of hypertension, atrial fibrillation, metabolic syndrome, and cognitive decline through sympathetic activation, HIF-1α induction, systemic inflammation, and oxidative stress. Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) is the first-line treatment, and pharmacological adjunct therapy is increasingly used for non-CPAP phenotypes (central, mixed, low arousal threshold).

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Upper airway collapse | Increased Pcrit (critical closing pressure) → collapse from negative pressure on inspiration | Increased AHI, apnoea/hypopnoea |
| Intermittent hypoxia | Repeated SpO2 desaturation → ↑HIF-1α → VEGF, EPO | Sympathetic activation, nocturnal hypoxia |
| Sympathetic hyperactivation | ↑NE secretion → vasoconstriction, increased HR/BP | Hypertension, risk of atrial fibrillation |
| Systemic inflammation | Elevated IL-6, CRP, TNF-α → endothelial dysfunction | Atherosclerosis, cardiovascular disease |
| Insulin resistance | HIF-1α → increased glucocorticoids, ↑lipolysis | Increased HOMA-IR, metabolic syndrome |
| Sleep fragmentation | Arousal response → decreased slow-wave/REM sleep | Daytime sleepiness (ESS), cognitive decline |
| Obesity-OSA vicious cycle | ↑Body fat → upper airway fat deposition → ↑Pcrit | Mutual worsening of obesity and OSA |

## Drug Targets

- **CPAP**: maintains upper airway patency via continuous positive pressure; reduces AHI by 95% or more, lowers blood pressure
- **Modafinil/solriamfetol** (wake-promoting agents): block dopamine reuptake/increase NE → improve residual excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS)
- **Tirzepatide** (dual GLP-1/GIPR agonist): weight loss in obese OSA → significant reduction in AHI (SURMOUNT-OSA)
- **Eszopiclone** (non-benzodiazepine hypnotic): increases arousal threshold → improves AHI in some OSA phenotypes
- **Acetazolamide** (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor): reduces loop gain in central apnoea/mixed OSA phenotypes

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: modafinil/solriamfetol oral, 2 compartments each, tirzepatide SC 3 compartments, eszopiclone/acetazolamide oral, 2 compartments each; Pcrit (critical upper airway pressure) · upper airway loop gain (LG) · arousal threshold (AT) · AHI · SpO2 · HIF-1α · sympathetic nerve activity (SNA) · systolic blood pressure · heart rate · CRP · HOMA-IR · ESS · body weight
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated (AHI=35) ② CPAP 10 cmH2O ③ CPAP+modafinil 200mg ④ CPAP+solriamfetol 150mg ⑤ tirzepatide 10mg QW (non-CPAP) ⑥ acetazolamide+eszopiclone (non-CPAP phenotype)
- **Calibration/rationale**: referenced against literature data from SURMOUNT-OSA (tirzepatide), TONES (solriamfetol), and CPAP AHI response studies

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① **Patient Profile** (baseline AHI · BMI · presence of hypertension · OSA phenotype settings), ② **PK** (plasma drug concentration-time curves), ③ **Key PD Measures** (trends in AHI · SpO2 · Pcrit · sympathetic nerve activity), ④ **Clinical Endpoints** (changes in ESS · blood pressure · HOMA-IR · CRP), ⑤ **Scenario Comparison** (direct comparison of 6 treatment strategies), ⑥ **Biomarkers** (trends in HIF-1α · body weight · heart rate · sleep fragmentation).
