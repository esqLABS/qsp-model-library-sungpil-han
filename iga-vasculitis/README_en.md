## Overview
IgA vasculitis (IgAV, formerly Henoch-Schönlein purpura/HSP) is a systemic vasculitis
arising from deposition of IgA1 immune complexes in small vessel walls. It is the most
common systemic vasculitis in children, with an annual incidence of about 10-22 per
100,000 children; it is rare in adults but follows a more severe course. It is
characterized by the four cardinal clinical manifestations of palpable purpura (100%),
arthralgia (75%), abdominal pain (65%), and nephritis (40-50%). The key pathogenic
mechanisms are Gd-IgA1-based immune complex formation, complement (alternative
pathway) activation, endothelial damage, and neutrophil/macrophage influx; treatment
uses corticosteroids and, for severe renal involvement, immunosuppressants (MMF,
cyclophosphamide, rituximab).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways
| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Gd-IgA1 immune complex formation | Galactose-deficient IgA1 + autoantibody (IgG) → IC formation | Deposition in the vessel wall |
| Complement alternative pathway activation | C3, C5a generation, MAC formation | Endothelial damage · increased vascular permeability |
| Neutrophil infiltration | Neutrophil influx via C5a/IL-8, NET formation | Leukocytoclastic vasculitis |
| Cutaneous small-vessel vasculitis | IgA/C3 deposition in skin, dermal small-vessel damage | Palpable purpura |
| Renal involvement | Mesangial IgA deposition, ANCA-like response | Hematuria · proteinuria · renal failure |
| Intestinal involvement | Intestinal small-vessel inflammation/hemorrhage | Abdominal pain · intussusception · hematochezia |
| Joint involvement | Synovial small-vessel inflammation | Arthralgia · arthritis |

## Key Drug Targets
- **Corticosteroids** (prednisolone): broad immunosuppression, NF-κB inhibition, relief of intestinal/joint symptoms
- **Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)**: inhibits B-cell proliferation → reduces autoantibody/IgA production, for severe nephritis
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): B-cell depletion for severe or relapsing nephritis
- **Cyclophosphamide**: for severe renal involvement with rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis
- **Dapsone**: used as adjunct therapy for cutaneous vasculitis
- **ACEi/ARB**: management of proteinuria and blood pressure

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (prednisolone 3-compartment, MMF 3-compartment, rituximab 3-compartment + CD20 binding) + disease PD compartments (Gd-IgA1, autoantibodies, immune complexes, complement C3/C5, mesangium, renal inflammation, proteinuria, eGFR, cutaneous purpura, gastrointestinal inflammation, BAFF, IL-6, TNF, fibrosis)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural course (untreated), ② steroid monotherapy (low-to-moderate dose), ③ MMF + steroid, ④ rituximab alone (severe nephritis), ⑤ steroid + MMF + ACEi combination
- **Calibration/evidence**: parameters calibrated against the SHARE initiative recommendations, the KDIGO 2021 pediatric vasculitis guidelines, and single-center cohort study data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (age, weight, presence of renal
involvement), drug PK concentration trends (steroid, MMF, rituximab), key PD
biomarkers (IgA, complement, CD20 B cells), clinical endpoints (purpura resolution,
proteinuria, eGFR), treatment scenario comparison, and long-term renal function
prediction.

## Usage
```r
library(mrgsolve)
mod <- mread("igav_mrgsolve_model.R")
out <- mrgsim(mod, end = 365)
plot(out)
# Shiny dashboard:
shiny::runApp("igav_shiny_app.R")
```
