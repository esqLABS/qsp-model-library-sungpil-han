## Overview

Diverticular disease is a condition in which small pouches (diverticula) form in the
colon wall, and it is closely linked to a Westernized diet pattern. Diverticula are
found in about 60% of Westerners over age 60, of whom about 25% progress to
diverticulitis. The key pathogenic mechanisms are increased intraluminal pressure and
weakened bowel-wall structure from a low-fiber diet, gut microbial imbalance
(dysbiosis), and reduced mucosal defense, with bacterial overgrowth within the
diverticula triggering inflammation via the NF-κB pathway. Treatment consists of a
high-fiber diet, cyclic rifaximin, mesalamine (5-ASA), and antibiotics during acute
episodes.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Increased intraluminal pressure | Low fiber → reduced luminal volume → excessive segmental contraction | Diverticulum formation (mucosal herniation) |
| Weakened bowel-wall structure | Collagen abnormality, defects in the muscularis layer | Increased diverticulum count |
| Gut microbial imbalance | Reduced protective bacteria, overgrowth of pathogenic bacteria → LPS generation | Reduced mucosal defense |
| NF-κB/inflammatory pathway | LPS → TLR4 → NF-κB → TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β | Peridiverticular inflammation, elevated CRP |
| Mucosal damage | Reduced butyrate → decreased mucosal epithelial regeneration | Persistent chronic inflammation |
| Visceral hypersensitivity | Chronic inflammation → hypersensitive visceral sensory nerves | Abdominal pain, IBS-like symptoms |
| Acute complications | Diverticular perforation, abscess, fistula, obstruction | Requires hospitalization/surgery |

## Key Drug Targets

- **Rifaximin**: gut-selective antibiotic → suppresses pathogenic bacteria, reduces LPS generation
- **Mesalamine (5-ASA)**: inhibits NF-κB → relieves mucosal inflammation, prevents recurrence
- **High-fiber diet/psyllium**: lowers intraluminal pressure, promotes growth of protective bacteria
- **Ciprofloxacin + metronidazole**: antimicrobial treatment for acute diverticulitis
- **Probiotics**: restore the gut microbial ecosystem

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: dietary fiber concentration, intraluminal pressure, mucosal defense, protective/pathogenic bacterial density, LPS, NF-κB, TNF-α/IL-6/IL-1β, neutrophils, CRP, diverticulum count, chronic inflammation, drug (rifaximin/mesalamine/cipro/metro) PK compartments, visceral hypersensitivity, collagen
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural course (Western diet), ② high-fiber diet (psyllium +15 g/day), ③ cyclic rifaximin (400 mg TID × 7 days/month), ④ mesalamine maintenance therapy (1.6 g/day), ⑤ acute diverticulitis — ciprofloxacin + metronidazole, ⑥ combination therapy (high fiber + rifaximin + mesalamine)
- **Calibration/evidence**: calibrated against the PREVENT rifaximin study, the DIVER mesalamine trial, and epidemiological data from Strate et al.

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① Patient Profile (dietary fiber intake, BMI, baseline diverticulum count settings), ② PK tab (antibiotic/mesalamine blood concentrations), ③ Microbiome/Inflammation PD tab (protective/pathogenic bacteria, NF-κB, cytokine trends), ④ Clinical Endpoints (diverticulum count, CRP, diverticulitis risk), ⑤ Scenario Comparison (simultaneous comparison of 6 treatment strategies), ⑥ Biomarkers (LPS, visceral hypersensitivity, mucosal defense trends).
