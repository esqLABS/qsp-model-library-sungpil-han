## Overview
Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is a chronic functional bowel disorder characterized by
abdominal pain and altered bowel habits without an organic cause, with a worldwide
prevalence of about 11%. It is a multifactorial disorder driven by the complex
interaction of brain-gut axis abnormalities, visceral hypersensitivity, abnormal
intestinal motility, low-grade mucosal inflammation, gut microbial imbalance
(dysbiosis), and increased intestinal permeability. It is divided into four subtypes:
constipation-predominant (IBS-C), diarrhea-predominant (IBS-D), mixed (IBS-M), and
unclassified (IBS-U). Treatment includes dietary modification (low FODMAP),
neuromodulators (tricyclic antidepressants, SSRIs), serotonergic agents (alosetron,
tegaserod), and gut-specific drugs (linaclotide, lubiprostone).

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways
| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Brain-gut axis dysfunction | CRF/stress → HPA axis hyperactivation, enteric nervous system hypersensitivity | Worsened abdominal pain/bloating |
| 5-HT (serotonin) signaling abnormality | Abnormal 5-HT secretion from intestinal EC cells, reduced SERT function | Impaired motility/secretion/visceral sensation regulation |
| Visceral hypersensitivity | Increased TRPV1/TRPA1 expression, spinal sensitization | Lowered pain threshold |
| Increased intestinal permeability | Abnormal tight-junction proteins (occludin, claudin), mast cell activation | Influx of bacterial components (LPS)/low-grade inflammation |
| Gut microbial imbalance | Altered Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, reduced SCFA production | Impaired motility/immune/neural regulation |
| Intestinal motility abnormality | Dysregulation of 5-HT4/5-HT3 receptors and chloride channels | Classification into IBS-D/IBS-C subtypes |

## Key Drug Targets
- **Linaclotide / Plecanatide** (GC-C agonists): raise cGMP → promote intestinal fluid secretion, suppress pain (IBS-C)
- **Lubiprostone** (chloride channel activator): increases intestinal fluid secretion (IBS-C)
- **Alosetron** (5-HT3 antagonist): inhibits colonic motility, suppresses visceral sensation (severe IBS-D)
- **Tricyclic antidepressants (TCA)** (amitriptyline): modulates visceral pain, inhibits 5-HT/NE reuptake
- **Rifaximin** (non-absorbable antibiotic): corrects gut microbial imbalance (IBS-D, non-constipation type)
- **Mebeverine / Hyoscine butylbromide** (antispasmodics): relax intestinal smooth muscle, relieve abdominal pain

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: brain-gut axis module (stress, CRF, cortisol), serotonin module (intestinal 5-HT, SERT occupancy), mast cell activation, inflammation, intestinal permeability, microbiome (dysbiosis), SCFA, visceral hypersensitivity, intestinal motility, and PD output compartments for pain/bloating/defecation discomfort + drug PK compartments (GC-C agonist 2-compartment, rifaximin 1-compartment, alosetron 2-compartment)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural course (untreated), ② linaclotide (IBS-C), ③ alosetron (IBS-D), ④ short-course rifaximin, ⑤ TCA + psychological therapy combination
- **Calibration/evidence**: based on key phase 3 clinical data including the LINACLOTIDE-301/302 trials, the TARGET 1/2 trials (alosetron), and the TARGET 3 trial (rifaximin)

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (subtype, baseline symptom severity, stress
level), drug PK concentration trends, brain-gut axis PD biomarkers (5-HT, CRF,
cortisol), clinical endpoints (IBS-SSS score, abdominal pain, change in bowel-movement
frequency), treatment scenario comparison, and subtype-specific response prediction.
