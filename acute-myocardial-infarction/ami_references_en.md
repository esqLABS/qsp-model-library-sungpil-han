# Acute myocardial infarction (STEMI) QSP model — references
## Acute Myocardial Infarction · Annotated Reference List

This list is arranged **by mechanism block**, so that where each cluster of `ami_qsp_model_en.dot` and
each parameter of `ami_mrgsolve_model.R` came from can be traced. The square brackets after each entry
mark what that reference actually fixes in the model — which structure, or which
numerical value, it sets.

> **How to read this.** A ★ marks a reference that directly determined the skeleton of this model. The rest
> fix the order of magnitude of a parameter, or supply a clinical observation the model has to
> reproduce. **The points the model cannot reproduce, or where it disagrees with the literature, are
> collected separately in §12** — not hiding them is the rule of this repository.

## 14 · Where this model disagrees with the literature

The value of a QSP model lies less in where it is right than in **stating where it is wrong**. Below are
the disagreements found by running `ami_reference_check.py`; they are left as they stand rather than
smoothed over.

| # | The model's prediction | Literature | How it was handled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | mPTP inhibition (CsA) given immediately before reperfusion reduces the infarct (experiment 5) | Piot 2008 [75] positive, **CIRCUS [76] and CYCLE [77] neutral** | The model does not claim that CsA "works". Experiment 5 shows that the predicted effect is **non-monotonic in ischaemic time**, and offers a **structural explanation** for how a pragmatic trial enrolling a wide band of ischaemic times dilutes its own effect size. This is not a defence of the neutral results but a statement of the conditions under which a neutral result and a positive pilot study can both be true. |
| 2 | An SGLT2 inhibitor reduces the 6-month EDV (experiment 9) | **The primary composite endpoint of EMPACT-MI [83] was neutral** (heart failure hospitalisation fell) | The model's SGLT2 effect consists of only two terms, volume (`GVOL_S`) and dilatation gain (`0.15×sglt`), and both act on **surrogates of remodelling**. The model does not compute a composite endpoint that includes death, so the model and EMPACT-MI are measuring different things. It must not be read as the model having "predicted" the trial. |
| 3 | Raising the intensity of anti-IL-1β weakens the scar and worsens dilatation (experiment 11) | Hammerman 1983 [45] confirmed this for steroids, but **there is no signal of scar weakening in CANTOS [46] or COLCOT [47]** | The model's U-shaped response appears only when IL-1β is blocked **almost completely** (`CAN_EMAX > 0.95`). Clinical doses of canakinumab or colchicine do not reach that region, and the model predicts no harm there either. The right-hand limb of the U is therefore **an extrapolation that has not been approached clinically**, and it is labelled as such. |
| 4 | With complete occlusion at 24–48 h, about 80% of the AAR is necrotic and an epicardial rim survives | The dog experiment of Reimer 1977 [1] gives about 75%. Human data are spread widely, 60–90%, depending on how the AAR is defined | Calibrated to the dog data. Human collaterals are poorer than the dog's, so lowering `COLL` (the 0.02–0.05 rows of experiment 3) makes the surviving rim disappear. The between-species difference has been absorbed into a single parameter, and that is **an assumption, not a result.** |
| 5 | The lysis–PCI crossover point lies at a PCI-related delay of about 60–120 min | Nallamothu [59] 60 min; Pinto [60] 40–180 min depending on the patient group | Consistent, but the model's crossover point is sensitive to three constants, `KLYS_PL`·`KREG`·`KPCI`. These were back-calculated to match patency rates (90-minute TIMI 3 ≈ 60% vs ≈ 95%), so the crossover point is closer to **a restatement of the patency data than an independent prediction**. This is stated explicitly in the description of experiment 6. |
| 6 | Clinical event rates such as mortality and heart failure hospitalisation | Stone 2016 [92], Eitel 2014 [93] | **The model does not compute event rates.** The endpoint nodes of cluster 15 are there to show the causal path on the map; the outputs of the R model go as far as IS, MVO, EF, EDV, BNP and scar strength. The step from a surrogate to an event rate is **not implemented** in this model. |
| 7 | The intracranial haemorrhage index of experiment 13 | Real ICH risk models (age · weight · blood pressure · previous stroke) | The `ICH index` of experiment 13 is **a reporting index** built from plasmin AUC and age alone, not a validated risk prediction model. It must not be read as an absolute risk. It is labelled as such in the code and in the output. |
| 8 | **Beta-blockade alone** **worsens** 6-month remodelling (experiment 9: growth 380%, untreated 205%) | In CAPRICORN [the 80 series], BHAT and others, post-infarct beta-blockade **improves** mortality and remodelling | **This is where the model is wrong.** In this model the only route for a beta-blocker is a reduction in heart rate and contractility, and that lowers cardiac output and so raises RAAS drive (Ang II 2.65 → 3.13). Correct as acute haemodynamics, but the opposite of the late outcome. The reason is that the model has no **antiarrhythmic, antioxidant, β1 receptor upregulation-recovery or direct reverse-remodelling** effect of beta-blockade. Combined with an ACE inhibitor (ACEi alone 61.7% → ACEi+BB 48.2%) the direction becomes right, so the defect is confined to the monotherapy trajectory in the absence of RAAS blockade. It is left as it stands rather than corrected. |
| 8b | The **magnitude** of infarct reduction by intravenous beta-blockade (model 2.0%) | METOCARD-CNIC [78] reports about a 20% reduction (25.6 vs 32.0 g) | **The direction and the timing are right but the magnitude is small.** In this model the only route by which rate control reaches the wavefront is **lengthened diastole → increased collateral flow** (`KDIAST`). Because basal metabolic demand does not depend on heart rate (which is correct by definition), a reduction in demand cannot rescue tissue that is already below basal. The model also lacks the **inhibition of neutrophil–platelet aggregation** mechanism that the Ibanez group reported later. `KDIAST` could have been raised to match the 20%, but that would be fitting a parameter to a trial result, so it was not done. |
| 8c | MVO fails to predict the 6-month EDV **independently** of infarct size (an EDV difference of 2.5 mL between MVO 3.17 and 13.33 %LV) | de Waha 2017 [34] and Eitel 2014 [93]: MVO remains an independent prognostic factor after adjustment for infarct size | **This is a point the model does not reproduce.** The *occurrence* of MVO separates well from infarct size (MVO/infarct ratio 0.23–0.96). The problem is *persistence*: with `KMVO_R` = 0.012/h (half-life about 2.4 days) MVO resolves before it reaches the time scale of remodelling, and the model has no irreversible route such as **iron deposition from intramyocardial haemorrhage** or **permanent capillary rarefaction**. Adding such a route would be expected to reproduce it, but `KMVO_R` was not arbitrarily lowered in order to fit the data. |
| 8d | **IL-1β blockade produces no effect at all** (sweeping both the time of administration and the intensity of blockade leaves scar, thinning, EDV and EF unchanged) | CANTOS [46] reduced cardiovascular events, COLCOT [47] reduced them too, and Abbate [49] suppressed CRP | **A plain negative result, and a structural defect.** The chain that builds the scar in the model is M1 → M2 → TGF-β → myofibroblast → collagen, and **it does not pass through IL-1β.** IL-1β contributes only 20% of MMP activation, so blocking it does not move the dynamics. To reproduce the clinical effect, IL-1β would need routes acting directly on (i) maintenance of M1 and delay of the M2 switch, (ii) negative inotropy in cardiomyocytes and (iii) apoptosis in the border zone. The experiment is not deleted but **reported as a negative result**. |
| 8e | Peak troponin does not rank infarct size **in the wrong order** (39.7 → 50.2 → 53.5, the same order as the actual infarcts) | Katus 1991 [89] and Chia 2008 [91]: a reperfused infarct gives a higher peak from a smaller infarct | **The time axis is reproduced, the magnitude axis is not.** The *time* of the peak is brought forward by reperfusion, 6.1 → 3.1 hours (correct). The *height* of the peak preserves the order of the actual infarct sizes (unlike the observation). AUC/infarct also changes 2-fold, 135 → 68, so AUC too underestimates the large infarct. The cause is presumed to be `WASH_FLOOR = 0.15` — 15% of the marker is released even from occluded myocardium, so the contrast does not open up enough. It was not lowered in order to match. |
| 9 | The **absolute position** of the remodelling separatrix | A critical infarct size of about 18–20 %LV [50, 55, 56] | The model's **untreated** separatrix lies at about 5 %LV and its **full GDMT** separatrix at about 18–20 %LV. Because the figures quoted come from guideline-treated cohorts, the treated separatrix agrees. Whether the untreated separatrix being that low is the actual natural history of the pre-ACE-inhibitor era, or a calibration error in the `KDIL`/`KHYP_E` ratio, cannot be distinguished from this model alone. **It has not been calibrated against data that would separate the two readings.** |
| 10 | The volume and BNP values of the divergent branch (EDV 600–1100 mL, BNP 70–730) | Real ventricles do not grow that large; the patient dies first, or another mechanism intervenes | The model has **neither death nor any other saturating mechanism.** `EDVMAX` (450 mL) is not biology but **a numerical ceiling**, and integration stops there. A trajectory past the separatrix reports **a verdict** — "this ventricle does not stabilise" — not a number of millilitres. Every affected row of the output is marked `DIVERGED` together with the day it was reached. |

## 15 · What the model deliberately leaves out

- **The spatial structure of the coronary anatomy.** The area at risk is a single scalar, `AAR`; there is no distinction between LAD/RCA/LCx and no multivessel disease.
- **Right ventricular infarction.** RV involvement in inferior infarction, and its characteristic preload-dependent haemodynamics, are absent.
- **Comorbidities such as diabetes and chronic kidney disease.** There are only three covariates, `AGE`·`PRECOND`·`COLL`.
- **Reinfarction and plaque progression.** Present on the map but not in the ODEs.
- **Between-individual variation in platelet count and coagulation factors**, and differences in P2Y₁₂ response by genotype (CYP2C19 and the like).
- **The dynamics of arrhythmia.** Cluster 13 exists only on the map and not in the ODEs — VF is an event, not a state variable, and does not fit the time scale of this model (hours–months).

This list exists in order to settle **what must not be asked** of the model.
