## Overview

Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) is a chronic allergic skin disease with a global prevalence of roughly 1–2%, in which wheals, flare, and itch persist for 6 weeks or longer without a clear external trigger. Its core pathogenic mechanism is spontaneous activation of cutaneous mast cells by FcεRI-bound IgE or autoantibodies (anti-FcεRI, anti-IgE), which release histamine and cytokines (IL-31, IL-33, TSLP) to drive the urticarial triad of wheal, flare, and itch. Elevated serum total IgE and positivity for thyroid peroxidase (TPO) autoantibodies define particular phenotypes. H1-antihistamines (first line), omalizumab (second line), and dupilumab (third line/most recently approved) make up the standard treatment ladder.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| IgE/FcεRI cross-linking | Autoantigen-IgE → FcεRI multimerisation → mast cell degranulation | Release of histamine, prostaglandins, leukotrienes → wheal and flare |
| Autoantibody pathway | Anti-FcεRIα/anti-IgE IgG → complement activation → Mas-related GPR X2 | Non-immunologic MC activation |
| Type 2 cytokines | IL-4 · IL-13 · IL-31 · IL-33 · TSLP → Th2/ILC2 amplification | Chronic inflammation, IgE class switching |
| JAK-STAT pathway | IL-4/IL-13 → JAK1/TYK2 → STAT6 → increased FcεRI expression | Increased mast cell sensitivity |
| Neuroimmune cross-talk | IL-31 → OSMRβ/IL-31RA → C fibres | Itch |
| BTK signalling | IgE-FcεRI → BTK → PLCγ → IP3/DAG → Ca²⁺ influx | Core degranulation step — BTKi target |
| RANKL/IL-17 (secondary) | Eosinophils · Th17 → soft-tissue remodelling | Chronic skin barrier damage |

## Key Drug Targets

- **H1-antihistamines (cetirizine, bilastine, fexofenadine)**: H1R inverse agonists; block mast-cell degranulation → suppress itch and wheal
- **Omalizumab (anti-IgE)**: binds free IgE → reduces FcεRI expression and armed mast cells; evidence from the ASTERIA and GLACIAL trials
- **Ligelizumab (anti-IgE, high affinity)**: ~50-fold higher IgE affinity than omalizumab; in phase 3 development
- **Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα)**: blocks the shared IL-4/IL-13 receptor; approval based on the LIBERTY-CSU CUPID A/B trials (2023 NEJM)
- **Ligelizumab/vercelizumab (2nd-generation anti-IgE)**: high-affinity IgE blockade for faster IgE suppression
- **BTK inhibitors (remibrutinib, evobrutinib)**: target BTK → block downstream FcεRI signalling; convenience of oral dosing
- **Cyclosporine**: inhibits calcineurin in mast cells/T cells; used in severe CSU refractory to antihistamines
