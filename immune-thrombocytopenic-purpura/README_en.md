## Overview
Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) is an autoimmune bleeding disorder caused by antiplatelet autoantibodies that accelerate platelet destruction and suppress megakaryocyte-driven platelet production. Prevalence is about 9.5 per 100,000 adults, with a bimodal age distribution in children and women over 60. Antiplatelet antibodies (mainly anti-GP IIb/IIIa and anti-Ib/IX) opsonise platelets, promoting their destruction by macrophages in the spleen and liver; the mechanism also includes dysregulated T-cell immune control (increased Th17/decreased Treg) and direct platelet destruction by cytotoxic T cells. Treatment follows a stepwise approach: first-line (corticosteroids, IVIG), second-line (TPO receptor agonists, rituximab), and third-line (splenectomy, FcRn inhibitors, Syk inhibitors).

## Key Pathways
| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Antiplatelet antibody generation | B-cell activation (supported by Th2/Th17), anti-GP IIb/IIIa IgG production | Platelet opsonisation |
| Splenic destruction | Phagocytosis of opsonised platelets by FcγR+ macrophages | Reduced platelet count |
| Megakaryocyte suppression | Antiplatelet antibodies inhibit megakaryocyte maturation and platelet release | Impaired production |
| Treg/Th17 imbalance | Decreased Treg, increased Th17 → dysregulated B-cell control | Sustained autoantibody production |
| TPO feedback failure | Insufficient TPO rise despite thrombocytopenia (cleared in the spleen) | Failed compensatory production |
| Cytotoxic T cells | Direct platelet destruction | Mechanism in antibody-negative ITP |

## Drug Targets
- **Corticosteroids** (prednisolone): suppress FcγR expression, inhibit antibody production, and reduce splenic destruction (first-line)
- **IVIG / anti-D immunoglobulin**: saturate/block FcγR, transiently suppress platelet destruction (emergency use)
- **TPO receptor agonists** (romiplostim, eltrombopag): promote megakaryocyte proliferation and platelet production (second-line)
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): depletes B cells, suppresses autoantibody production (second-line)
- **Fostamatinib (Syk inhibitor)**: inhibits macrophage FcγR signalling, blocks splenic destruction (third-line)
- **Efgartigimod / nipocalimab (FcRn inhibitors)**: shorten IgG half-life → deplete antibodies (third-line)

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)
- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (prednisolone, 1 compartment; IVIG, 1 compartment; romiplostim, 2 compartments; rituximab, 2 compartments; fostamatinib, 2 compartments; efgartigimod, 1 compartment; eltrombopag, 1 compartment) + disease PD compartments (platelets, splenic platelets, TPO, megakaryocyte progenitors and megakaryocytes, autoantibodies, B cells, Treg, Th17, macrophages)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① natural history, ② steroids alone, ③ IVIG + steroids, ④ romiplostim alone, ⑤ eltrombopag alone, ⑥ rituximab alone, ⑦ fostamatinib, ⑧ efgartigimod
- **Calibration/rationale**: parameters set from the EXTEND trial (eltrombopag), romiplostim phase 3 trials, and ASH 2019 ITP guideline data

## Shiny Dashboard
Consists of tabs for patient profile input (baseline platelet count, bleeding severity, prior treatment history), drug PK concentration trends, platelet/megakaryocyte/autoantibody PD biomarkers, clinical endpoints (achieving a platelet count ≥30 × 10⁹/L, bleeding risk), treatment scenario comparison, and long-term remission prediction.
