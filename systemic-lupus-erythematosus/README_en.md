## Overview

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a chronic autoimmune disease characterised by type I interferon (IFN-α/β) hyperactivation and multi-organ damage from anti-double-stranded-DNA (anti-dsDNA) antibodies and immune complexes. Its global prevalence is about 20-150 per 100,000, more than 10 times higher in women of childbearing age. It affects the kidneys (lupus nephritis), skin, nervous system, haematological system, cardiovascular system, and other organs throughout the body, following a relapsing-remitting course. Antimalarials (hydroxychloroquine) form the foundation of therapy for all SLE, and belimumab (anti-BAFF) and anifrolumab (anti-IFNAR) are approved as targeted therapies added on top of standard immunosuppressive treatment.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecule/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Type I IFN hyperactivation | pDC TLR7/9 → IFN-α/β → ISG upregulation → amplified immune activation | Sustained systemic inflammation, accelerated organ damage |
| Anti-dsDNA and immune-complex formation | Apoptotic chromatin → autoantibodies (anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm) → IC formation | IC deposition in kidney, skin, vessels |
| Complement activation | IC → C1q/C3a/C5a → glomerular injury, neutrophil recruitment | Lupus nephritis, vasculitis |
| B-cell hyperactivation (BAFF pathway) | BAFF overexpression → B-cell survival, plasma-cell differentiation → mass autoantibody production | Rising anti-dsDNA titre, complement consumption |
| Tfh-plasma-cell axis | IL-21/IL-6 → Tfh → germinal-centre response → maturation of high-affinity autoantibodies | Sustained/worsening autoimmunity |
| NETosis pathway | Low-density granulocytes (LDG) → NETosis → dsDNA release → pDC activation | Positive feedback on IFN-α secretion |
| Regulatory T-cell (Treg) deficiency | Reduced Treg number/function → failure to suppress autoreactive T cells | Breakdown of immune tolerance, relapse |

## Drug Targets

- **Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ)**: inhibits TLR7/9/IFN-α; foundational therapy for all SLE, prevents flares and improves long-term prognosis
- **Corticosteroids**: broad anti-inflammatory/immunosuppressive action; short-term control of acute flares and organ involvement
- **Mycophenolate/azathioprine/cyclophosphamide**: inhibit cell proliferation; lupus-nephritis induction and maintenance
- **Belimumab**: anti-BAFF → inhibits B-cell survival; approved for active, seropositive SLE
- **Anifrolumab**: anti-IFNAR1 → blocks type I IFN signalling; approved for moderate-to-severe active SLE
- **Voclosporin/tacrolimus**: calcineurin inhibition → suppresses Th-cell activation; lupus nephritis

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: PK compartments for HCQ/belimumab/anifrolumab/MMF/cyclophosphamide + type I IFN production dynamics, pDC activation compartment, BAFF/B-cell/plasma-cell modules, anti-dsDNA titre compartment, complement C3/C4 dynamics, lupus-nephritis GFR module, and a SLEDAI score estimation compartment
- **Key treatment scenarios**: untreated natural course, HCQ alone, prednisolone, MMF, azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, belimumab, anifrolumab, HCQ+belimumab, HCQ+anifrolumab, MMF+belimumab+HCQ triple combination, and many others
- **Calibration/rationale**: based on clinical data from BLISS-52/-76 (belimumab), TULIP-1/-2 (anifrolumab), AURA-LV/AURORA (voclosporin), and ALMS (MMF vs. IV-CYC for lupus nephritis)

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: (1) **Patient Profile** — anti-dsDNA/complement baseline, organ involvement, SLEDAI settings; (2) **PK Profile** — plasma concentration over time for biologics/small molecules; (3) **PD Key Measures** — IFN score, BAFF, anti-dsDNA suppression dynamics; (4) **Clinical Endpoints** — SLEDAI, BILAG, GFR, proteinuria over time; (5) **Scenario Comparison** — remission rate and organ-damage comparison across treatment strategies; (6) **Biomarkers** — anti-dsDNA, complement C3/C4, IFN signature score, blood counts, proteinuria.
