## Overview

Giant cell arteritis (GCA) is a granulomatous vasculitis of the large
vessels (temporal artery, aortic branches) occurring in people over 50,
with an incidence of roughly 10-30 per 100,000 in that population. Key
symptoms are temporal artery tenderness, headache, jaw claudication, and
blindness (anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy), and prompt steroid
treatment is essential to prevent blindness. In its pathophysiology,
dendritic cells in the vessel wall present antigen, activating Th1/Th17
cells; macrophages and giant cells then infiltrate the vessel wall,
producing IL-6 overexpression and VEGF-mediated angiogenesis. Tocilizumab
(anti-IL-6R) is effective for steroid tapering.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Dendritic cell antigen presentation | Vessel-wall DC -> CD4+ T-cell activation | Promotes Th1/Th17 differentiation |
| Th17/IL-17 pathway | IL-17A -> neutrophil influx, vascular inflammation | Elevated ESR/CRP, vascular damage |
| IL-6 overexpression | Macrophage-derived IL-6 -> acute-phase response | Elevated ESR/CRP, systemic symptoms |
| Macrophage/giant cell granuloma | Infiltration of the adventitia and media -> intimal hyperplasia | Vascular occlusion, ischaemia |
| VEGF-mediated angiogenesis | Th17 -> VEGF -> neovascularisation | Accelerates intimal hyperplasia |
| Corticosteroid response | GR -> NF-κB suppression -> reduced cytokines | Rapid symptom improvement |
| Bone density loss | Long-term steroids -> reduced BMD | Osteoporosis, fracture risk |

## Key Drug Targets

- **High-dose steroids** (prednisolone 40-60 mg/day): immediate suppression of inflammation, prevention of blindness — first-line therapy
- **Tocilizumab**: anti-IL-6R -> blocks IL-6 signalling -> allows steroid tapering (GiACTA trial)
- **Methotrexate**: adjunct for steroid tapering, prevention of relapse (small RCTs)
- **Abatacept**: CTLA4-Ig -> blocks T-cell co-stimulation -> under investigation for maintenance therapy
- **Zanubrutinib (BTK inhibitor)**: under investigation as a new therapeutic target

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: drug PK compartments (prednisolone GI/plasma/
  tissue, tocilizumab SC depot/central/peripheral, sIL-6R/IL-6 free/bound
  TMDD compartments) + PD compartments (free/bound IL-6, CRP, ESR,
  dendritic cell activation, macrophage activation, Th17, VEGF, bone
  density, cumulative steroid dose)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① GC alone, slow taper (GiACTA control arm),
  ② GC alone, rapid taper (GiACTA placebo arm), ③ tocilizumab IV 8 mg/kg
  q4w + GC slow taper (GiACTA arm 1), ④ tocilizumab SC 162 mg qw + GC slow
  taper (GiACTA arm 2), ⑤ tocilizumab SC 162 mg q2w + GC slow taper
  (GiACTA arm 3)
- **Calibration/basis**: the GiACTA trial (Stone et al. NEJM 2017),
  tocilizumab IL-6R PK/PD literature, and reference to models of the
  ESR/CRP acute-phase response

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① patient profile (set baseline ESR/CRP, VDI damage
index, bone density), ② PK tab (prednisolone/tocilizumab blood
concentrations and TMDD), ③ immune PD tab (trends in IL-6, sIL-6R, CRP,
ESR, macrophage activity), ④ clinical endpoints (remission maintenance
rate, number of relapses, cumulative steroid dose), ⑤ scenario comparison
(simultaneous comparison of the 5 GiACTA treatment strategies), ⑥
biomarkers (trends in Th17, VEGF, bone density, cumulative GC dose).
