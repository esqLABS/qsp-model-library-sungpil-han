## Overview

Sjögren's syndrome is a systemic autoimmune disease in which CD4+ T lymphocytes and B cells infiltrate the exocrine glands — chiefly the salivary and lacrimal glands — causing chronic dryness (dry mouth and dry eyes). Its prevalence is roughly 0.1–0.6%, and it occurs overwhelmingly in women at a ratio of 9:1. The core pathogenesis involves overactive type I interferon (IFN-α/β) signalling and B-cell hyperactivation (production of RF and anti-SSA/Ro and anti-SSB/La autoantibodies). Its major long-term complication is a roughly 15–20-fold increased risk of lymphoma (predominantly MALT type) relative to healthy individuals. No disease-modifying therapy is currently approved; hydroxychloroquine, pilocarpine, and cevimeline are used for symptomatic treatment, and immunosuppressants and rituximab are used off-label for systemic involvement.

## Key Pathophysiological Pathways

| Pathway | Key molecules/mechanism | Clinical outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Type I IFN pathway | pDC TLR7/9 → excess IFN-α/β production → ISG expression → glandular injury | Endothelial and exocrine gland dysfunction |
| B-cell hyperactivation | BAFF overexpression → B-cell survival/proliferation → RF and anti-SSA/SSB autoantibodies | Immune complex deposition, complement activation |
| CD4+ T-cell (Th1/Tfh) infiltration | IFN-γ · IL-21 → lymphocytic focus formation within salivary glands | Progressive exocrine gland destruction |
| BAFF/APRIL pathway | BAFF overexpression → B-cell differentiation, sustained autoantibody production | Increased risk of B-cell lymphoma |
| Muscarinic receptor inhibition | Anti-M3R autoantibodies → suppressed salivary/lacrimal secretion | Xerostomia, xerophthalmia |
| Renal and neural involvement | Immune complexes → tubular acidosis, nerve sheath involvement | Hypokalaemia, peripheral neuropathy |
| Lymphomagenesis pathway | Chronic B-cell activation → MALT lymphoma clonal expansion | Fatal complication |

## Key Drug Targets

- **Hydroxychloroquine**: inhibits TLR9/IFN-α; relieves fatigue, arthralgia, and mild systemic symptoms
- **Pilocarpine/cevimeline**: M3R agonists → stimulate salivary/lacrimal secretion; symptomatic treatment of dry mouth and dry eyes
- **Rituximab**: anti-CD20 → B-cell depletion → reduced autoantibodies; indicated for vasculitis, nephritis, and neural involvement
- **Belimumab**: anti-BAFF → suppresses B-cell survival; pSS indication under clinical investigation
- **JAK inhibitors (baricitinib, upadacitinib)**: inhibit JAK1/2 → suppress type I IFN and IL-6 signalling; in clinical trial stage
- **Abatacept**: blocks CD80/86 → suppresses Tfh and plasma cell activation; some positive results in phase 2 pSS trials

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: rituximab/belimumab/hydroxychloroquine PK compartments + type I IFN production dynamics, BAFF and B-cell count modules, autoantibody (anti-SSA) titre compartment, salivary gland function prediction module (USFR, unstimulated salivary flow rate), and ESSDAI score estimation compartment
- **Key treatment scenarios**: no treatment/natural course, hydroxychloroquine, pilocarpine, methotrexate, azathioprine, single-dose rituximab, repeated rituximab, belimumab, baricitinib, rituximab + belimumab combination, and others
- **Calibration/evidence base**: based on TRACTISS (rituximab RCT), BELISS (belimumab), EULAR pSS recommendations, and clinical cohort data using the Vitali classification criteria

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: (1) **Patient profile** — sets anti-SSA/SSB status, baseline ESSDAI, and lymphocytic focus score; (2) **PK profile** — plasma concentrations of rituximab/belimumab/small molecules; (3) **Key PD markers** — IFN score, BAFF, and B-cell count suppression dynamics; (4) **Clinical endpoints** — time course of ESSDAI, ESSPRI, salivary flow, and dry-eye score; (5) **Scenario comparison** — comparison of glandular function and systemic activity across treatment strategies; (6) **Biomarkers** — anti-SSA, IgG, complement C3/C4, IFN signature score.
