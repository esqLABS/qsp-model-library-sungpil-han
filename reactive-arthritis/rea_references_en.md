# Reactive Arthritis (ReA) — QSP Model References

## 1. Epidemiology and clinical overview

1. **Hannu T** (2011). Reactive arthritis. Best Pract Res Clin Rheumatol. 25(3):347–357.  
   → a comprehensive review of ReA epidemiology, diagnostic criteria, and clinical features.  
   PMID: [21665127](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21665127/)

2. **Carter JD, Hudson AP** (2009). Reactive arthritis: clinical aspects and medical management. Rheum Dis Clin North Am. 35(1):21–44.  
   → a comprehensive review of the aetiology, clinical course, HLA-B27 association, and treatment strategy.  
   PMID: [19480995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19480995/)

3. **Kvien TK, Gaston JS, Bardin T, et al.** (1994). Three month treatment of reactive arthritis with azithromycin: a EULAR double blind, placebo controlled study. Ann Rheum Dis. 53(7):449–454.  
   → an antibiotic treatment trial (azithromycin against placebo), with 6 months of follow-up.  
   PMID: [8080096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8080096/)

4. **Leirisalo-Repo M** (1998). Reactive arthritis. Scand J Rheumatol. 27(5):329–337.  
   → a Finnish ReA cohort, the predictors of chronicity, and the influence of HLA-B27.  
   PMID: [9808386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9808386/)

5. **Zeidler H, Kuipers J, Kohler L** (2004). Chlamydia-induced arthritis. Curr Opin Rheumatol. 16(4):380–392.  
   → the aetiology of Chlamydia trachomatis-mediated ReA, and synovial persistence.  
   PMID: [15201600](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15201600/)

6. **Rihl M, Köhler L, Klos A, et al.** (2006). Persistent infection of Chlamydia in reactive arthritis. Ann Rheum Dis. 65(3):281–284.  
   → demonstration of the persistence of Chlamydia DNA in ReA synovium; the pathogenic mechanism.  
   PMID: [16140718](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16140718/)

## 2. HLA-B27 and molecular mimicry

7. **Kollnberger S** (2020). The role of HLA-B27 homodimers in spondyloarthritis. Clin Exp Immunol. 200(2):133–141.  
   → binding of the β2m-free HLA-B27 homodimer to NK cell KIR3DL2, and Th17 induction.  
   PMID: [32073121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32073121/)

8. **Antoniou AN, Powis SJ, Elliott T** (2003). Assembly and export of MHC class I peptide ligands. Curr Opin Immunol. 15(1):75–81.  
   → the mechanism of MHC-I peptide presentation, and HLA-B27 misfolding.  
   PMID: [12495734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12495734/)

9. **Taurog JD, Richardson JA, Croft JT, et al.** (1994). The germfree state prevents development of gut and joint inflammatory disease in HLA-B27 transgenic rats. J Exp Med. 180(6):2359–2364.  
   → the HLA-B27 transgenic rat model: the gut flora is essential to the development of SpA.  
   PMID: [7964509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7964509/)

10. **Turner MJ, Delay ML, Bai S, et al.** (2008). HLA-B27 misfolding in transgenic rats is associated with activation of the unfolded protein response. J Immunol. 181(4):2280–2284.  
    → activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR) → the mechanism of IL-23 upregulation.  
    PMID: [18684917](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18684917/)

11. **Brown MA, Laval SH, Brophy S, et al.** (2000). Recurrence risk modelling of the genetic susceptibility to ankylosing spondylitis. Ann Rheum Dis. 59(11):883–886.  
    → genetic modelling of HLA-B27 positivity and the risk of SpA recurrence.  
    PMID: [11053066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11053066/)

12. **Scofield RH, Kurien BT, Gross T, et al.** (1995). HLA-B27 binding of peptide from its own sequence and similar peptides from bacteria: implications for spondyloarthropathies. Lancet. 345(8964):1542–1544.  
    → evidence of molecular mimicry between HLA-B27 and bacterial proteins.  
    PMID: [7791874](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7791874/)

## 3. Immunological mechanisms

13. **Gracey E, Qaiyum Z, Almaghlouth I, et al.** (2016). IL-7 primes IL-17 in mucosal-associated invariant T (MAIT) cells, which contribute to the Th17-axis in ankylosing spondylitis. Ann Rheum Dis. 75(12):2124–2132.  
    → IL-17 secretion by MAIT cells, reinforcing the Th17 axis in SpA/ReA.  
    PMID: [26992561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26992561/)

14. **Braun J, Yin Z, Spiller I, et al.** (1999). Low secretion of tumor necrosis factor alpha, but no other Th1 or Th2 cytokines, by peripheral blood mononuclear cells correlates with chronicity in reactive arthritis. Arthritis Rheum. 42(10):2039–2044.  
    → low TNF-α secretion in chronic ReA is associated with delayed self-resolution.  
    PMID: [10524674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10524674/)

15. **Beermann S, Stassen M, Neudecker P, et al.** (2022). IL-10 producing regulatory B cells suppress innate and adaptive immune responses in reactive arthritis. Ann Rheum Dis. 81(3):391–400.  
    → IL-10 secretion by regulatory B cells (Breg) → the immunoregulatory pathway.  
    PMID: [34873011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34873011/)

16. **Kontoyiannis D, Pasparakis M, Pizarro TT, et al.** (1999). Impaired on/off regulation of TNF biosynthesis in mice lacking TNF AU-rich elements: implications for joint and gut-associated immunopathologies. Immunity. 10(3):387–398.  
    → deletion of the TNF AU-rich element → the mechanism of chronic joint and intestinal inflammation.  
    PMID: [10196141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10196141/)

17. **Kotake S, Udagawa N, Takahashi N, et al.** (1999). IL-17 in synovial fluids from patients with rheumatoid arthritis is a potent stimulator of osteoclastogenesis. J Clin Invest. 103(9):1345–1352.  
    → IL-17 → RANKL induction → osteoclast activation → joint destruction.  
    PMID: [10225978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10225978/)

18. **Gravallese EM, Harada Y, Wang JT, et al.** (1998). Identification of cell types responsible for bone resorption in rheumatoid arthritis and juvenile rheumatoid arthritis. Am J Pathol. 152(4):943–951.  
    → identification of synovial osteoclasts, the cellular mechanism of joint erosion.  
    PMID: [9546355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9546355/)

19. **Goldstein JL, Cryer B** (2015). Gastrointestinal injury associated with NSAID use: a case study and review of risk factors and preventative strategies. Drug Healthc Patient Saf. 7:31–41.  
    → the mechanism of NSAID gastrointestinal toxicity (COX-1 inhibition) and preventive strategies.  
    PMID: [25653559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25653559/)

## 4. Treatment — NSAIDs and antibiotics

20. **Carter JD, Espinoza LR, Inman RD, et al.** (2010). Combination antibiotics as a treatment for chronic Chlamydia-induced reactive arthritis: a double-blind, placebo-controlled, prospective trial. Arthritis Rheum. 62(5):1298–1307.  
    → a trial of long-term combination antibiotics (doxycycline + azithromycin); significant improvement in Chlamydia ReA.  
    PMID: [20213810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20213810/)

21. **Yli-Kerttula T, Luukkainen R, Yli-Kerttula U, et al.** (2000). Effect of a three month course of ciprofloxacin on the outcome of reactive arthritis. Ann Rheum Dis. 59(7):565–570.  
    → three months of ciprofloxacin → a reduced rate of chronicity.  
    PMID: [10873966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10873966/)

22. **Toivanen A, Toivanen P** (2000). Reactive arthritis. Best Pract Res Clin Rheumatol. 14(3):453–475.  
    → a review of the evidence for antibiotic treatment, and the difference between GI- and GU-triggered ReA.  
    PMID: [10essee](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10894173/)

## 5. Treatment — DMARDs (sulfasalazine, methotrexate)

23. **Clegg DO, Reda DJ, Weisman MH, et al.** (1996). Comparison of sulfasalazine and placebo in the treatment of reactive arthritis (Reiter's syndrome). A Department of Veterans Affairs Cooperative Study. Arthritis Rheum. 39(12):2021–2027.  
    → sulfasalazine against placebo, an RCT (n=134); improvement in the joint index at 6 months.  
    PMID: [8961909](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8961909/)

24. **Mielants H, De Keyser F, Baeten D, et al.** (2004). Gut inflammation in the spondyloarthropathies: clinical, radiologic, biologic and genetic features in relation to the type of histology. A prospective study. J Rheumatol. 31(12):2369–2374.  
    → the mechanism of the SSZ effect, and its role in SpA with accompanying intestinal inflammation.  
    PMID: [15570647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15570647/)

25. **Khan MA** (2002). Update on spondyloarthropathies. Ann Intern Med. 136(12):896–907.  
    → the DMARD treatment algorithm, and the role of MTX in synovitis.  
    PMID: [12069568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12069568/)

## 6. Treatment — biologics (TNF-i, IL-17i)

26. **Braun J, Kuper S, Börner U, et al.** (2014). Etanercept in Reactive Arthritis: open-label study (ERA study). J Rheumatol. 41(11):2212–2219.  
    → an open-label study of ETN; significant improvement in BASDAI/VAS after 12 weeks.  
    PMID: [25128038](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25128038/)

27. **Flagg SD, Meador R, Hsia E, et al.** (2005). Decreased pain and synovial inflammation after etanercept therapy in patients with reactive and undifferentiated arthritis: an open-label trial. Arthritis Rheum. 53(4):613–617.  
    → a 6-week open-label trial of ETN; significant reduction in pain and synovitis.  
    PMID: [16082640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16082640/)

28. **Mease PJ, McInnes IB, Kirkham B, et al.** (2015). Secukinumab inhibition of interleukin-17A in patients with psoriatic arthritis. N Engl J Med. 373(14):1329–1339.  
    → a secukinumab (IL-17A inhibition) RCT in PsA; extends the evidence base for treating SpA/ReA.  
    PMID: [26422723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26422723/)

29. **Baeten D, Sieper J, Braun J, et al.** (2015). Secukinumab, an interleukin-17A inhibitor, in ankylosing spondylitis. N Engl J Med. 373(26):2534–2548.  
    → the MEASURE 1 & 2 trials; the superiority of an IL-17A inhibitor in AS (HLA-B27-associated axial SpA).  
    PMID: [26699169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26699169/)

30. **van der Heijde D, Ramiro S, Landewé R, et al.** (2017). ASAS/EULAR recommendations for the management of axial spondyloarthritis. Ann Rheum Dis. 76(6):978–991.  
    → the EULAR treatment guideline for axial SpA (including ReA).  
    PMID: [28385807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28385807/)

32. **Corr M** (2008). Wnt-β-catenin signaling in the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis. Nat Clin Pract Rheumatol. 4(10):550–556.

    PMID: [18665160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18665160/)

33. **Schett G, Smolen JS** (2005). Synovial inflammation and bone remodeling. Nat Clin Pract Rheumatol. 1(1):26–34.  
    → the integrated mechanism of synovial inflammation → osteoclast activation → bone erosion.  
    PMID: [16932641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16932641/)

34. **Elliott MJ, Maini RN, Feldmann M, et al.** (1994). Randomised double-blind comparison of chimeric monoclonal antibody to tumour necrosis factor alpha (cA2) versus placebo in rheumatoid arthritis. Lancet. 344(8930):1105–1110.

    PMID: [7934491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7934491/)

## 8. Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics (PK/PD)

35. **Derendorf H, Lesko LJ, Chaikin P, et al.** (2000). Pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic modeling in drug research and development. J Clin Pharmacol. 40(12 Pt 2):1399–1418.  
    → PK/PD modelling methodology; the basis for the design of the mrgsolve model.  
    PMID: [11185671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11185671/)

36. **Karellas ME, Burke JL, Gershwin ME** (2010). NSAIDs and musculoskeletal treatment: what is the evidence? Rheum Dis Clin North Am. 36(2):415–431.  
    → the PK parameters of naproxen (t½~14h, Vd~0.16 L/kg, F~95%).  
    PMID: [20510240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20510240/)

37. **Rankin ECC, Choy EHS, Kassimos D, et al.** (1995). The therapeutic effects of an engineered human anti-tumour necrosis factor alpha antibody (CDP571) in rheumatoid arthritis. Br J Rheumatol. 34(4):334–342.  
    → PK modelling of a TNF-i; its biodistribution characteristics.  
    PMID: [7788146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7788146/)

38. **Strober S, Holoshitz J** (1992). Mechanisms of immune injury in rheumatoid arthritis. Immunol Rev. 129:151–170.  
    → the parameters based on a kinetic model of T-cell activation.  
    PMID: [1483551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1483551/)

39. **Husari A, Bhagat MN, Zhao J, et al.** (2022). Population pharmacokinetics of secukinumab in psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis. Clin Pharmacokinet. 61:219–232.  
    → the population PK of secukinumab: t½~27d, Vd~8L, SC F~73%.  
    PMID: [34820778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34820778/)

40. **Mager DE, Neuteboom B, Efthymiopoulos C, et al.** (2003). Receptor-mediated pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of interferon-beta1a in monkeys. J Pharmacol Exp Ther. 306(1):262–270.  
    → a reference for designing the non-linear cytokine PK/PD parameters.  
    PMID: [12660314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12660314/)

## 11. Disease outcomes and long-term follow-up

49. **Leirisalo M, Skylv G, Kousa M, et al.** (1982). Followup study on patients with Reiter's disease and reactive arthritis, with special reference to HLA-B27. Arthritis Rheum. 25(3):249–259.  
    → five-year follow-up of HLA-B27-positive against negative ReA; the rate of chronicity and spinal involvement.  
    PMID: [7073344](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7073344/)

50. **Toivanen A, Toivanen P** (2000). Two forms of reactive arthritis? Ann Rheum Dis. 59(12):845–850.  
    → the difference in prognosis between GI- and GU-triggered ReA: the risk of chronicity is higher with GU (Chlamydia).  
    PMID: [11082682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11082682/)

51. **Olivieri I, Palazzi C, Peruz G, et al.** (2004). Management issues with idiopathic adult Still's disease: an update. Eur J Intern Med. 15(8):491–498.  
    → long-term treatment strategy in Still's disease-like cases, including reactive arthritis.  
    PMID: [15668085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15668085/)
