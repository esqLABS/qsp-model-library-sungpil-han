# Bile Acid Diarrhoea / Malabsorption (BAM)

> **The feedback loop's sensor measures the bile acid that was "absorbed", but the symptom is
> produced by the bile acid that "overflowed".**
> At steady state, the amount passing into the colon is **exactly equal to the rate of hepatic
> synthesis** (mass conservation).
> So malabsorption **cannot, by itself, raise the colonic load by even 1%.**
> What raises the load is **the feedback response that malabsorption unleashes.**
>
> So this disease is written with **two** numbers — `φ` (the surviving ileal ASBT absorptive
> capacity) and `κ` (the ileal FGF19 sensor gain) — and the two tests used clinically each
> read **a different one of the two.**

## 1. Why two numbers

The enterohepatic circulation is a feedback loop. But this loop has an **asymmetry**.

```
        What the sensor measures:   absorbed flux    =  f · C          (ileal FXR → FGF19)
        What produces the symptom:  overflow flux     =  (1-f) · C      (colonic bile-acid load)
```

At steady state, mass conservation is enforced — the overflow amount eventually all leaves in
the stool, and that must equal the amount the liver newly synthesises:

```
        colonic load  ≡  faecal loss  ≡  S  (rate of hepatic synthesis)
```

And the signal the sensor sees is `κ · x · S`, where `x = f/(1-f)`. Combining the two equations
reduces the entire loop to **a single closed-form line**:

```
                        S_max
        S  =  ───────────────────────── ,      x = f/(1-f)
               1 + (a · κ · x · S)^h
```

`φ` enters only into `x`, via `f`, and `κ` enters only into the sensor gain. **The two axes are
independent.**

**With the feedback frozen, cutting `φ` to one-twentieth still moves the colonic bile-acid load

> Malabsorption is real in both columns. **Diarrhoea appears only in the column where the liver
> is allowed to respond.** This is the central claim of this model, and it is tested with a
> single switch (`FEEDBACK = 0`).

## 3. Two lesions, two tests — why SeHCAT and C4 see different things

⚙ Derivation: when only `φ` changes, the turnover rate `k = S/P = (1-f)·n` changes, so
**SeHCAT moves**. When only `κ` changes, synthesis `S` and the pool `P` grow **together**, so
the turnover rate stays nearly the same — **SeHCAT stays normal and only C4 and faecal bile
acids rise.**

| `φ` | `κ` | SeHCAT % | C4 | FGF19 | Faecal BA | Faecal water | Stools/day | Interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1.00 | 1.00 | 50.45 | 17.5 | 247 | 606 | 129 | 1.09 | Normal |
| 1.00 | 0.45 | 36.46 | 33.3 | 126 | 1148 | 293 | 2.59 | Pure sensor lesion |
| 1.00 | 0.25 | **27.74** | **47.4** | 74 | 1634 | 432 | 3.89 | Severe sensor lesion |
| 0.35 | 1.00 | 2.75 | 41.7 | 125 | 1438 | 376 | 3.36 | Type 1, feedback normal |
| 0.35 | 0.45 | 1.21 | 64.7 | 65 | 2226 | 598 | 5.46 | Type 1 + sensor lesion |
| 0.10 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 81.3 | 50 | 2795 | 745 | 6.88 | Extensive ileal disease |
| 0.10 | 0.45 | 0.00 | 98.2 | 25 | 3372 | 876 | 8.15 | Worst case |

With the ileum **structurally intact** (`φ` = 1.00), if only the sensor gain drops from 1.00 to
0.25:

| | Change |
|---|---|
| SeHCAT | 50.45 → 27.74 % (−45 %) — **still normal (>15 %)** |
| Serum C4 | 17.5 → 47.4 ng/mL (**+170 %**, near the cut-off of 48) |
| Faecal water | 129 → 432 mL/day (**+236 %**) |

> ⚙ **SeHCAT-negative bile acid diarrhoea is not a test failure — it is a structural prediction
> of the model.** The test that sees such patients is faecal bile acids / C4.

**Combination is synergistic:** observed −58.3% vs the Bliss-independence expectation of
−53.1%. ⚙ Because the FXR agonist restores the signal that the resin had abolished.

**Only the FXR agonist lowers `S` itself** (faecal BA 2048 → 1404 → 497). The resin **raises**
faecal BA (2048 → 2074): the symptom improves, but the driving force grows.

⚙ **A testable prediction:** FXR agonists raise the SeHCAT value without repairing `φ` at all
(0.14 → 0.24%, 0.38% for tropifexor), because the pool turnover rate `S/P` falls. The price is
a rise in LDL-C (83 → 95 → 135 mg/dL), in the same direction as OCA's known lipid effect.

### 4-1. The mirror image — an ASBT inhibitor is a deliberate `φ` lesion

Give elobixibat to a normal gut and the entire BAM phenotype should come out. That is exactly
why elobixibat is **a treatment for chronic constipation**, so this is a falsifiability test.

| elobixibat mg/day | Faecal water | Stools/day | C4 | FGF19 | SeHCAT % | Ds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 129 | 1.09 | 17.5 | 247 | 50.45 | 1.16 |
| 5 | 484 | 4.37 | 53.3 | 161 | 0.78 | 2.76 |
| 10 | 543 | 4.93 | 60.7 | 158 | 0.30 | 3.00 |
| 15 | 575 | 5.24 | 64.9 | 156 | 0.17 | 3.13 |

The direction, mechanism, and biomarkers (C4 rising, FGF19 falling, SeHCAT collapsing) are all
correct. **The magnitude is an overprediction** — real elobixibat increases stools by 5–6 per
week, not by 4 per day (see §11).

## 6. The 100 cm rule — partial success, reported honestly

There are only three inputs: the distal skew of ASBT density (decay length 60 cm, an
anatomical fact), the CYP7A1 compensatory ceiling (6-fold), and the effective critical
micellar concentration of 1.5 mM. **No resection outcome data were used at all.**

| Resected cm | `φ` | Pool (g) | Peak [BA] mM | CYP7A1 fold | Faecal fat g/d | SeHCAT % | Phenotype |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 1.000 | 3.10 | 7.98 | 0.87 | 3.6 | 50.45 | Bile acid diarrhoea |
| 20 | 0.717 | 2.32 | 5.95 | 1.16 | 4.9 | 29.48 | Bile acid diarrhoea |
| 40 | 0.513 | 1.80 | 4.58 | 1.53 | **7.8** | 12.42 | **Steatorrhoea** |
| 60 | 0.368 | 1.44 | 3.65 | 1.98 | 13.1 | 3.45 | Steatorrhoea |
| 100 | 0.189 | 1.07 | 2.68 | **3.02** | 27.6 | 0.10 | Steatorrhoea |
| 180 | 0.050 | 0.88 | 2.13 | **4.82** | 46.2 | 0.00 | Steatorrhoea |

**What the model got right ⚙**
- That a **tipping point exists**. A point where the phenotype changes **qualitatively** from
  bile acid diarrhoea to steatorrhoea appears even though no parameter was put in to make it.
- The mechanism. The compensatory ceiling is real (CYP7A1 rises up to 4.8-fold and can go no
  further), and the moment it hits the ceiling, the pool can no longer keep the duodenal
  bile-acid concentration above the CMC.
- The clinical consequence. Beyond that point, a resin takes away bile acid the duodenum can no
  longer spare, worsening steatorrhoea — reproducing the textbook contraindication.

**What the model got wrong ⚠**
- The **location** of the tipping point. The model puts the transition at **34 cm**, while
  clinical textbooks say 100 cm — about 3-fold too proximal.
- Only two parameters govern this location: the effective CMC and the ASBT decay length λ.
  Lowering the CMC from 1.5 to about 1.1 mM moves the tipping point close to 100 cm. But doing
  so would collapse the very claim that "this was derived, not fitted" — so **it was not
  fitted.**
- This is therefore left as a falsifiable quantitative statement: **if the effective CMC of
  mixed micelles is truly 1.5 mM, the tipping point should sit near 35 cm, not 100 cm.** One of
  the two is wrong.

## 7. Cholecystectomy (Type 3) — a timing lesion, not an absorptive lesion

| Condition | SeHCAT % | C4 | Ds | Faecal water | Stools/day | Cycles/day | f/pass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Normal gallbladder | 50.45 | 17.5 | 1.16 | 129 | 1.09 | 4.06 | 0.9645 |
| **Cholecystectomy alone** | 47.16 | 17.1 | 1.11 | **123** | **1.04** | 4.65 | 0.9662 |
| Cholecystectomy + `φ` 0.60 | 22.87 | 26.9 | 1.72 | 213 | 1.85 | 3.66 | 0.9144 |
| Cholecystectomy + `κ` 0.45 | 29.98 | 32.5 | 2.03 | 284 | 2.50 | 5.03 | 0.9491 |
| `φ` 0.60 alone (comparison) | 19.47 | 27.3 | 1.76 | 223 | 1.94 | 3.95 | 0.9118 |

⚙ **Removing the gallbladder alone does not produce bile acid diarrhoea** (faecal water
129 → 123, a slight decrease if anything). The number of cycles rises from 4.06 to 4.65, but
`f` stays the same, so the steady-state load does not change. Bile acid diarrhoea after
cholecystectomy needs **a second hit**. This is consistent with the clinical fact that the
majority of cholecystectomy patients do not develop diarrhoea.

## 9. Where the model disproved its own hypothesis — the transit-absorption feedback

The hypothesis at design time was this: there is a **positive** feedback in which *colonic bile
acids speed up transit → ileal contact time shortens → outflow rises → transit speeds up
again*, and if the gain exceeds 1, a transient insult (infectious enteritis) could **lock in**
as a chronic state.

The loop was opened (`TRANS` fixed) and the two halves were measured separately:

| `φ` | aTR | Ds @T=1.00 | Ds @T=1.10 | A = dDs/dT | B = dT/dDs | **L = A×B** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00 | 1.55 | 1.246 | 1.181 | **−0.649** | 0.273 | **−0.18** |
| 1.00 | 3.60 | 1.246 | 1.181 | −0.649 | 0.633 | −0.41 |
| 0.30 | 1.55 | 3.766 | 3.494 | **−2.722** | 0.252 | **−0.69** |
| 0.30 | 3.60 | 3.766 | 3.494 | −2.722 | 0.586 | −1.60 |

**A is negative.** When transit speeds up, ileal outflow rises, but the effect of the
secretagogue being diluted and cleared faster in the colon overwhelms it. That is, this arm is
**not a positive but a negative feedback**. A direct test (recovery after 14 days of transient
enteritis) also returned fully to baseline at every gain value (Δ −0.11%).

> ⚙ **Conclusion (opposite to the hypothesis):** bile acid diarrhoea does **not** lock in. The
> transit arm is an amplifier, not a cause of disease, and post-infectious bile acid diarrhoea
> is not explained by this loop alone — there must be a residual lesion in `φ` or `κ`.
> Loperamide's benefit, too, comes not from "breaking a vicious circle" but from **lengthening
> the time available for water absorption**. The ileal-side claim did survive, though:
> loperamide also reduces faecal bile acids (2048 → 1924 µmol/day), because longer ileal
> contact time raises `f`.

## 11. Known limitations (⚠ what you must know before using this model)

1. **The location of the 100 cm rule is off by 3-fold** (§6). The mechanism and its existence
   are right, but it transitions at 34 cm. Lowering the effective CMC to 1.1 mM would fit it,
   but that would be fitting.
2. **Drug-effect magnitudes are generally overpredicted.** Tropifexor 90 µg lowers C4 by
   −75.8%, close to the measured value, but the faecal-water change of −81.7% is far larger
   than the improvement in bowel movements observed in clinical trials. The same is true for
   elobixibat (§4-1). The direction and the ranking are trustworthy, but **do not trust the
   absolute effect size.**
3. **The resin's "structural escape" is barely visible in this patient** (C4 +0.7%), because at
   `φ` = 0.20 CYP7A1 is already near its ceiling and has no room to rise further. The model
   predicts that **the escape will be largest in mild disease** — this is a testable
   prediction.
4. **The healthy-subject SeHCAT value of 50.5% sits at the top of the reference range.**
   Whole-body gamma counting also counts colonic contents, so the real test may read slightly
   higher than the model, whereas the model counts only the pure circulating pool.
5. **Metabolic effects of the microbiota, immunity, and bile-acid signalling** (TGR5–GLP-1,
   hepatic lipids) are present in the map but are included only minimally in the ODE.
6. This is a semi-quantitative model for education and research. **Do not use it for clinical
   decision-making.**
