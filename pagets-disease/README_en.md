## Overview

Paget's disease of bone (PBD) is a chronic metabolic bone disease in which bone remodeling at specific sites becomes abnormally accelerated, with a prevalence of about 2–3% in people of Northern European descent aged 50 and over. SQSTM1 (p62) gene mutations and paramyxovirus infection are the key risk factors; overactivated osteoclasts drive abnormal bone resorption, mixed with excessive bone formation by osteoblasts responding to it. The result is the formation of disorganised, remodelled woven bone, which causes bone deformity (skull enlargement, long bone bowing), pain, hearing loss, nerve compression, high-output heart failure, and other complications. Zoledronic acid (a single 5 mg IV dose) is currently the strongest first-line treatment.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| SQSTM1/NF-κB hyperactivation | SQSTM1 mutation → p62 aggregation → excess TRAF6/RANK signalling → ↑NF-κB | Osteoclast overactivation, abnormal bone resorption |
| RANKL overexpression | ↑RANKL in osteocytes/osteoblasts → ↑osteoclast differentiation/activity | Elevated bone resorption markers (CTX, NTX) |
| Increased osteoclast number | Megakaryocyte-like hyperplastic osteoclasts → excessive bone resorption | Several-fold elevation of bsALP · NTX |
| Reactive osteoblast activity | Excessive bone formation after resorption → generation of woven bone | ↑bsALP, abnormal bone structure |
| Vascular hyperplasia | ↑Vascularisation within Paget lesions → increased cardiac output | High-output heart failure in a minority of cases |
| Nerve/vascular compression | Enlarged bone → stenosis of the auditory canal/vertebral foramen | Hearing loss, nerve root compression |
| Pain mechanism | Periosteal stretching, microfractures, nerve compression | Localised pain, secondary joint degeneration |

## Drug Targets

- **Zoledronic acid**: third-generation N-bisphosphonate; inhibits the osteoclast mevalonate pathway (FPP synthase) → osteoclast apoptosis (single 5 mg IV dose, remission maintained for years; HORIZON-PDB)
- **Alendronate**: oral bisphosphonate; inhibits bone resorption by 60–70% (40 mg/day for 6 months)
- **Calcitonin (SC)**: osteoclast inhibition; historical standard treatment, now a second-line alternative since the advent of zoledronic acid
- **Denosumab**: anti-RANKL → blocks osteoclast differentiation; its role in PBD is under investigation
- **Supportive care**: adequate calcium/vitamin D supplementation; surgery (deformity correction, hearing aids)

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: zoledronic acid IV 2 compartments + bone binding, alendronate oral absorption/central/peripheral, calcitonin SC 2 compartments, denosumab SC + central + peripheral; RANKL · OPG, osteoclast precursors · osteoclasts · osteoblast precursors · osteoblasts, BMD, bsALP · NTX · CTX (bone turnover markers), pain score
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated ② zoledronic acid 5 mg IV single dose ③ alendronate 40 mg/day ×6 months ④ calcitonin 100 IU SC daily ×6 months ⑤ denosumab 60 mg SC Q6M ⑥ zoledronic acid + supportive care ⑦ sequential therapy: alendronate → zoledronic acid
- **Calibration/rationale**: referenced against data from HORIZON-PDB (zoledronic acid single 5 mg vs risedronate) and PRISM (Paget's disease follow-up)

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of 6 tabs: ① **Patient Profile** (number of affected sites · baseline ALP · pain score · SQSTM1 mutation status settings), ② **PK** (plasma and intraosseous drug concentration-time curves), ③ **Key PD Measures** (osteoclast/osteoblast activity, RANKL/OPG trends), ④ **Clinical Endpoints** (ALP/bsALP normalisation, NTX/CTX reduction, pain improvement), ⑤ **Scenario Comparison** (direct comparison of 7 treatment strategies), ⑥ **Biomarkers** (long-term trends in BMD/bone turnover markers).
