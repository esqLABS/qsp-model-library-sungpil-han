## Overview

Graves' disease is an autoimmune endocrine disease in which the thyroid is persistently overstimulated by autoantibodies (TRAb) that stimulate the TSH receptor (TSHR), producing hyperthyroidism. It is the most common cause of hyperthyroidism; prevalence is about 1 in 10 women, lower in men, and it occurs most often in the 30s-40s. When B cell-derived TRAb binds TSHR and persistently activates cAMP signalling, this produces the classic symptoms of T4/T3 overproduction, thyroid enlargement, and tachycardia. The characteristic ocular lesion (Graves' orbitopathy, GO) arises through combined IGF-1R/TSHR stimulation of orbital fibroblasts. Antithyroid drugs (methimazole, PTU), radioactive iodine (RAI), and surgery are the main treatment options.

## Key Pathways

| Pathway | Key Molecule/Mechanism | Clinical Outcome |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| TRAb formation | HLA-DR3/DQ2-restricted antigen presentation → B cell stimulation → secretion of stimulating TRAb IgG | Persistent TSHR stimulation |
| cAMP/PKA signalling hyperactivation | TSHR-Gs-adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → upregulation of TPO · NIS → T4/T3 oversynthesis | Hyperthyroidism |
| HPT axis suppression | Excess fT3 · fT4 → negative feedback on hypothalamus/pituitary | TSH suppression (<0.01 mIU/L) |
| Thyroid enlargement | cAMP → VEGF · IGF-1 → thyroid cell proliferation | Goitre, increased vascularity |
| Cardiovascular effects | Excess fT3 → increased heart rate · increased myocardial contractility · shortened QTc | Tachycardia, risk of atrial fibrillation |
| Graves' orbitopathy | TRAb + IGF-1R activation → GAG accumulation in orbital fibroblasts/adipocytes | Proptosis, diplopia |
| Effects on bone metabolism | Excess fT3 → increased osteoclast activity → bone resorption | Decreased bone density |

## Drug Targets

- **Methimazole (MMI)**: inhibits TPO (thyroid peroxidase) → blocks T4/T3 synthesis; first-line antithyroid drug
- **Propylthiouracil (PTU)**: TPO inhibition + inhibition of peripheral T4→T3 conversion (D1 inhibition); preferred in the first trimester of pregnancy and thyroid storm
- **Radioactive iodine (RAI, ¹³¹I)**: accumulation in the thyroid via NIS → destruction of thyroid cells by beta radiation
- **Propranolol**: non-selective β-blocker → immediate control of tachycardia/tremor + partial inhibition of T4→T3 conversion
- **Block-and-replace**: high-dose MMI + LT4 supplementation; stabilises thyroid function
- **Rituximab**: B cell depletion; used in severe refractory Graves' orbitopathy

## mrgsolve Model (ODE Model)

- **Compartment structure**: includes drug PK (MMI, PTU, RAI serum/intrathyroidal radiation dose, propranolol) + disease PD (TPO inhibition, thyroid mass, T4 · T3 · fT4 · fT3 · rT3 · TSH compartments, B cells, TRAb, bone resorption, heart rate, Graves' orbitopathy activity)
- **Key treatment scenarios**: ① untreated baseline, ② methimazole 30 mg/day, ③ radioactive iodine 15 mCi, ④ MMI + propranolol combination, ⑤ block-and-replace (MMI + LT4), ⑥ rituximab + MMI (refractory GO)
- **Calibration/rationale**: qualitatively calibrated the time course of TSH/fT4 normalisation based on data from Ross et al. (ATA 2016 guidelines), Cooper et al. (NEJM 2005), and Nakamura et al. (JCEM 2007)

## Shiny Dashboard

Consists of tabs for patient profile settings (baseline TRAb, fT4, thyroid size, GO severity), drug PK (MMI/RAI kinetics), thyroid hormone PD (TSH/fT4/fT3 trajectories), cardiovascular/bone clinical endpoints (heart rate, bone resorption markers), Graves' orbitopathy activity, comparison of 6 treatment scenarios, and biomarkers (TRAb/TSH/fT4).
